Rabi frequencies and coupling strengths

Transition amplitudes combine angular-momentum factors with beam geometry; Rabi frequencies add the intensity and the strength from the Einstein A coefficient. Species forms use zero-field states; structure forms include field-induced hyperfine mixing. See Conventions for matrix ordering and selection of $F$ labels.

Levels.clebsch_gordan — Method
clebsch_gordan(lower::StateSpec => upper::StateSpec)

Returns the bare Clebsch–Gordan coefficient $⟨j m; R Δm | j' m'⟩$ between two fine-structure states, with the rank $R$ the electric-multipole order from multipole_rank.

This is the purely geometric coupling factor; the general transition amplitude relative to the reduced matrix element — species-aware, covering hyperfine $F$ levels and their exact mixing at a static field — is transition_amplitude.

Levels.coupling_matrix — Method
coupling_matrix(species, basis::StateBasis, lower => upper, intensity, ε, direction)
coupling_matrix(s::Eigenstructure, lower => upper, intensity, ε, direction)

Returns the complex angular Rabi frequencies of rabi_frequency in the upper-row, lower-column block, zero elsewhere. The Hamiltonian in angular-frequency units is (C + C') / 2.

The species form uses the supplied coupled basis. The structure form uses basis(s) in the field eigenbasis, with complete-manifold amplitudes rotated before any selection of states. For hyperfine species each level argument may name a whole fine-structure manifold or select one $F$ label after rotation. Partial and reordered bases retain the corresponding matrix elements.

Levels.dipole_components — Method
dipole_components(species, basis::StateBasis, lower => upper)
dipole_components(s::Eigenstructure, lower => upper)

Returns three E1 electric-dipole operator matrices $d_q$ in coulomb metres, indexed by q + 2 for $q = -1, 0, +1$. Entries occupy the upper-row, lower-column block; all others are zero. Basis and $F$-label selection follow coupling_matrix. An E2 pair raises an error.

The reduced element is defined by

\[⟨J' m'|d_q|J m⟩ = ⟨J m; 1 q|J' m'⟩ \frac{⟨J'‖d‖J⟩}{\sqrt{2J'+1}}, \qquad \frac{⟨J'‖d‖J⟩}{\sqrt{2J'+1}} = \sqrt{\frac{3π ε_0 ħ c^3 A}{ω^3}},\]

with the fine-structure Einstein A coefficient and centroid frequency $ω$. The reduced scale is taken positive, and each entry is that scale times transition_amplitude. Rate data supplies no relative sign between the reduced elements of different pairs; coherent sums through several intermediate manifolds require a consistent choice of those signs.

For $\vec E(t) = \mathrm{Re}[\vec ε E_0 e^{-iωt}]$, with unit $\vec ε$ and $E_0 = \sqrt{2I/(ε_0 c)}$, the interaction $H_\mathrm{int} = -\vec d ⋅ \vec E$ gives the rotating-wave amplitude

\[Ω_\mathrm{int} = -\frac{E_0}{ħ}\sum_q g_q(ε)⟨u|d_q|l⟩, \qquad g_q(ε) = (-1)^q ε_{-q}.\]

rabi_frequency and coupling_matrix use the contraction with the overall minus sign omitted, a common phase shift of the field. For unit ε, their E1 entries therefore equal E0/ħ * sum(dipole_geometry(ε)[q+2] * d[q+2][u, l] for q in -1:1).

Levels.hyperfine_reduction — Method
hyperfine_reduction(nuclear_spin, lower => upper; rank)
hyperfine_reduction(species::HyperfineOneElectronSpecies, lower => upper; rank)

Returns the factor relating an electronic rank-k multipole amplitude between hyperfine states to its fine-structure reduced element:

\[β^{(k)}(F → F') = (-1)^{J' + I + F + k} \sqrt{(2F + 1)(2J' + 1)} \begin{Bmatrix} J & J' & k \\ F' & F & I \end{Bmatrix},\]

so $⟨F' m'|T^k_q|F m⟩ = ⟨F m; k q|F' m'⟩ β^{(k)} ⟨J'‖T^k‖J⟩/\sqrt{2J'+1}$. The coupled states use electron-first Condon–Shortley coefficients $⟨J m_J; I m_I | F m_F⟩$, as in Levels.coupling_transform and [Campbell2026], Appendix E. Nuclear-first states differ by $(-1)^{I+J-F}$ per state; amplitudes between them acquire the corresponding initial- and final-state phases.

For fixed upper $F'$, $\sum_F β^2 = 1$: $β^2$ gives the branching fractions, and every upper hyperfine sublevel has the fine-structure decay rate. Species tables therefore store fine-structure Einstein A coefficients. For $I = 0$, $β = +1$ on allowed transitions.

rank defaults to multipole_rank; an explicit rank supports other operators such as M1.

References

  • [Campbell2026]: W. C. Campbell, "Angular Geometry of Atomic Multipole Transitions", arXiv:2510.07451.

Levels.intensity_for_rabi — Method
intensity_for_rabi(species, lower::StateSpec => upper::StateSpec, Ω, ε, direction)
intensity_for_rabi(s::Eigenstructure, lower::StateSpec => upper::StateSpec, Ω, ε, direction)

Returns the intensity at which a running wave of polarisation ε and propagation direction direction drives the lower → upper component with the Rabi frequency magnitude abs(Ω) (an angular frequency) — the inverse of rabi_frequency, whose arguments and species/structure forms it shares, since the Rabi frequency scales as the square root of the intensity. Raises if the geometry does not drive the component at all.

Levels.multipole_rank — Method
multipole_rank(lower => upper)

Returns the electric-multipole rank of the transition between the two given levels — 1 (E1) if the level parities differ, 2 (E2) if they are equal — as determined from the orbital angular momenta. Magnetic multipoles and electric orders beyond E2 are not considered.

Levels.rabi_frequency — Method
rabi_frequency(species, lower::StateSpec => upper::StateSpec, intensity, ε, direction)
rabi_frequency(s::Eigenstructure, lower::StateSpec => upper::StateSpec, intensity, ε, direction)

Returns the complex angular Rabi frequency $Ω$ of a running wave with the given intensity, polarisation ε and propagation direction. The Hamiltonian coupling is $(ħ/2)(Ω |u⟩⟨l| + \mathrm{h.c.})$; an isolated resonant pair has excitation probability $\sin^2(|Ω|t/2)$.

Following [James1998],

\[Ω_\mathrm{E1} = \sqrt{\frac{6π c^2 I A}{ħ ω^3}} \, a_{ul} \, d_{Δm}, \qquad Ω_\mathrm{E2} = \sqrt{\frac{20π c^2 I A}{ħ ω^3}} \, a_{ul} \, Γ_{Δm},\]

where $a_{ul}$ is transition_amplitude, and $d_q$ and $Γ_q$ come from dipole_geometry and quadrupole_geometry. The species form uses zero-field amplitudes; the structure form includes field-induced $F$ mixing. Both use the fine-structure pair's Einstein A coefficient and centroid frequency $ω$ for the physical scale.

ε and direction are normalised internally; direction enters only for E2. The complex phase, including the phase of ε, is retained so matrices built from these values preserve interference between components. abs(Ω) gives the magnitude. dipole_components defines the interaction sign.

References

  • [James1998]: D. F. V. James, "Quantum dynamics of cold trapped ions with application to quantum computation", Appl. Phys. B 66, 181 (1998), doi:10.1007/s003400050373; Eqs. (5.11)–(5.13) in the arXiv:quant-ph/9702053 numbering.

Levels.rabi_normalised — Method
rabi_normalised(couplings, basis::StateBasis, transition::Pair, Ω0)

Scales the given relative coupling matrix such that the entry for the lower => upper transition has magnitude abs(Ω0) (its carrier Rabi frequency, an angular frequency; a complex rabi_frequency contributes its magnitude only), fixing the physical scale of all the couplings. A pure index operation: the matrix keeps its representation (pass basis(s) for a structure-form matrix), and its internally consistent relative phases.

An error is raised if the amplitude for the given transition (nearly) vanishes in couplings, as the normalisation would then be ill-defined.

Levels.reduction_factor — Method

Returns the multiplier of the rank-rank Clebsch–Gordan coefficient between two levels: the triangle condition (0 or 1) for fine-structure levels, or the Levels.hyperfine_reduction factor $β$ for hyperfine levels.

Levels.relative_amplitudes — Method
relative_amplitudes(species, source::ManifoldSolution, target::ManifoldSolution, rank)

Returns the [target, source] matrix of rank-rank transition amplitudes relative to the fine-structure reduced matrix element between the eigenstates of two manifold solutions (over their canonical bases): the complete-manifold zero-field matrix of transition_amplitude values, rotated into both field eigenbases. This is the one primitive every at-field coupling matrix, Rabi frequency, physical dipole component and decay operator selects from — a restriction to particular $F$ levels or states is always applied after this rotation. Definite $m$ makes each entry belong to a single component $q = m_{target} - m_{source}$.

Levels.relative_couplings — Method
relative_couplings(species, basis::StateBasis, lower => upper, ε, direction)
relative_couplings(s::Eigenstructure, lower => upper, ε, direction)

Returns the dimensionless complex E1 or E2 coupling amplitudes $c_{m→m'} = ⟨\mathrm{up}|T^k_q|\mathrm{lo}⟩_\mathrm{rel} \, g_{Δm}$ — the transition_amplitude times the normalised geometric channel amplitude (dipole_geometry or quadrupole_geometry) — in the upper⟨row|lower⟩⟨col| block, zero elsewhere: coupling_matrix without its physical scale. The species form is the zero-field matrix in the coupled basis, the structure form the at-field matrix in the field eigenbasis of s indexed by basis(s) (rotated as a whole fine-structure pair, then selected, like coupling_matrix). The overall scale is typically fixed by normalising one probed component to a known carrier Rabi frequency with rabi_normalised.

Levels.transition_amplitude — Method
transition_amplitude(species, lower::StateSpec => upper::StateSpec; rank)
transition_amplitude(s::Eigenstructure, lower::StateSpec => upper::StateSpec; rank)

Returns the dimensionless rank-k transition amplitude $⟨u|T^k_q|l⟩ / (⟨J'‖T^k‖J⟩/\sqrt{2J'+1})$, with $q = m_u - m_l$. rank defaults to multipole_rank; an explicit rank supports other operators, e.g. 1 for M1 within a fine-structure level.

The species form uses zero-field states: the Clebsch–Gordan coefficient $⟨J m; k q | J' m'⟩$ for fine structure, or $⟨F m; k q | F' m'⟩ β^{(k)}(F → F')$ for hyperfine structure, with $β$ from Levels.hyperfine_reduction.

The structure form rotates the complete-manifold tensor component into the field eigenbases before selecting the labelled states, including their $F$ mixing. Any state of coupled_basis(s) may be named. Signs follow the structure's eigenvector gauge; Conventions describes its zero-field limit and possible sign jumps in field sweeps.
