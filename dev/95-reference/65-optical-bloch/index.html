Optical Bloch equations

Levels.OpticalBloch — Module

Multi-level Lindblad master equations and their coupling to trapped-ion motion for laser-cooling calculations.

lindblad_model combines an Eigenstructure with a LaserScheme: the structure supplies the field eigenstates, the scheme supplies beams and a rotating-frame choice. The resulting LindbladModel contains the Hamiltonian, spontaneous-emission operators and laser phase diffusion. Beam couplings that cannot be made static in the chosen frame remain as beat-note harmonics.

motional_coupling adds a MotionalMode, giving projected Lamb–Dicke factors, sideband couplings and recoil data. With QuantumToolbox loaded, motional_model builds the internal ⊗ Fock master equation and cooling_metrics compares rate-equation, integrated-transient and Liouvillian-spectrum estimates. PhononRateEquation keeps the rate description valid for saturated sidebands, without a joint model. semiclassical_cooling_rates gives the drift of hot modes, jointly for many modes, from classical motion.

The solver-independent BandLiouvillian restricts motional coherences for numerical calculations. IntegratedTransientSolver uses a sparse linear solve for steady states and integral relaxation times. Hamiltonians use angular-frequency units and jump operators square-root rate units; see Conventions.

source

Loading QuantumToolbox.jl activates LevelsQuantumToolboxExt, which provides steadystate(model) and liouvillian(model) for internal and motional models, as well as motional_model, populations, mean_phonon_number and cooling_metrics (other than for SaturatedRates and SemiclassicalRates, which need no solver package). See also QuantumToolbox integration.

Internal-state model

Lasers and rotating frame

Levels.OpticalBloch.LaserBeam — Type

One running-wave laser beam.

The frequency is a RelativeFrequency — a detuning from the zero-field interval of a named pair of levels ($F$ levels for a hyperfine species), so the tabulated line centres never enter the model. ε and direction are the (complex) polarisation and the unit propagation direction in the frame with the quantisation axis along ẑ (cf. Levels.beam_vectors); only their directions matter, the field amplitude being fixed by intensity. linewidth is the Lorentzian full width at half maximum of the laser spectrum (angular units), modelled as phase diffusion (cf. lindblad_model).

Constructed via LaserBeam(frequency, intensity, ε, direction; linewidth), or with the level pair and detuning spelled out as LaserBeam(lower => upper, detuning, intensity, ε, direction; linewidth).

source
Levels.OpticalBloch.LaserScheme — Type

A collection of laser beams and a rotating-frame choice, applied to an Eigenstructure by lindblad_model.

Constructed with LaserScheme(beams; frame_reference, frame_kind). frame_kind selects one frame frequency per fine-structure level (:levels, default) or per state (:states); see RotatingFrame. frame_reference sets the root level's frame frequency to its zero-field centroid. It defaults to the upper level of the first beam, or the first basis level if there are no beams. Changing this reference leaves observables unchanged. Beams and reference levels are validated when the frame is built.

Levels.OpticalBloch.RotatingFrame — Type

The rotating frame of a LaserScheme.

Every basis state $i$ rotates at a frame frequency $ω_i$; the rotating-frame Hamiltonian then carries $E_i/ħ - ω_i$ on its diagonal, and a beam of frequency $ω$ coupling states $i$ (lower) and $k$ (upper) does so with the time dependence $e^{-i (ω - ω_k + ω_i) t}$. The frame frequencies are assigned along a breadth-first spanning tree rooted at the scheme's frame_reference so that this exponent vanishes on the tree, in one of two ways (LaserScheme(…; frame_kind)):

  • :levels (default): all Zeeman/hyperfine states of a fine-structure level share one frame frequency; the graph has the levels as nodes and the beams as edges (a beam with several polarisation components is one edge). This keeps every spontaneous-emission operator time-independent, whatever its grouping. Beams outside the tree — a second beam on an already connected level pair, or one closing a loop of beams — beat at the frequency mismatch.
  • :states: the graph has the basis states as nodes and the individual non-vanishing coupling components of the beams as edges, so beams of different frequency on the same level pair can still be absorbed into the frame as long as they couple disjoint state pairs (the σ⁺-pump/π-probe EIT configuration on $J = 1/2 → 1/2$). The Hamiltonian is then static whenever the component graph has no frequency-inconsistent loop. A spontaneous-emission operator grouping several components stays time-independent only if all its components see the same frame difference; lindblad_model checks this and asks for decay = :resolved otherwise.

offsets store $ω_i$ relative to the zero-field centroid of the state's level, so that the tabulated line centres cancel: across a tree edge the offset grows by the beam's detuning plus the zero-field hyperfine shifts of its reference levels. traversals[i, b] counts, signed (upwards positive), how often the tree path from the reference to state $i$ runs along beam $b$ — the coefficients with which each beam's frequency (and hence its phase noise) enters the state's frame. beats lists the coupling components (beam, lower, upper, w) left with a beat note $w = ω - (ω_k - ω_i)$ (w ≠ 0), which the LindbladModel keeps as harmonic terms; states no beam reaches keep their centroid as frame frequency.

Master equation and decay

Levels.OpticalBloch.lindblad_model — Function
lindblad_model(s::Eigenstructure, scheme::LaserScheme; decay = :grouped, open_channels = :error)

Assembles the LindbladModel of the scheme on the structure s — which fixes the species, the static field and the states kept — from the atomic data. All matrices use the structure's field eigenbasis and ordering.

Hamiltonian. The frame diagonal holds each state's energy at the static field relative to its level's zero-field centroid (Levels.offsets) minus the corresponding frame offset of rotating_frame; every beam adds $(C + C^†)/2$ with the complex Rabi frequencies of coupling_matrix over the two fine-structure manifolds it connects (all their $F$ levels for a hyperfine species) — into the static part for its time-independent components, as harmonic terms for those with a beat note.

Spontaneous emission. For every pair of basis levels with an Einstein A coefficient (rank $k$ from multipole_rank), one jump operator per emitted spherical component $q = -k…k$, $L_q = \sqrt{A} \sum_{m' - m = q} ⟨\mathrm{up}|T^k_q|\mathrm{lo}⟩_\mathrm{rel} \, |\mathrm{lo}⟩⟨\mathrm{up}|$ with the relative amplitudes of Levels.transition_amplitude (exact at the static field) — the grouping appropriate when the Zeeman (hyperfine) splittings are unresolved against the photon bandwidth, $\sum_q L_q^† L_q = A \, P_\mathrm{upper}$. decay = :resolved instead gives one operator per state pair (appropriate for well-resolved hyperfine structure; in a per-state frame — frame_kind = :states — it is also what makes an operator whose components see different frame differences time-independent, at the price of the coherence-transfer terms between them, and is then required). Omitting states truncates the model to the retained eigenstates: an upper level with decay channels to levels missing from the structure raises an error naming them, unless open_channels = :drop discards those channels (the level then lives longer than its physical lifetime); missing F levels or Zeeman states are treated by the same policy.

Laser linewidth. A beam with non-zero linewidth $γ$ (FWHM) adds the phase-diffusion operator $\sqrt{γ} \sum_i n_{b,i} P_i$ with $P_i$ the state projectors and $n_{b,i}$ the signed traversal counts of the beam on the frame tree. The coherence between states $i$ and $j$ decays at $(1/2) ∑_b γ_b (n_{b,i} - n_{b,j})^2$, independently of the frame reference. Linewidths of beams outside the frame tree are not representable this way and raise an error.

Levels.OpticalBloch.LindbladModel — Type

The Lindblad master equation of the internal states of an Eigenstructure under a LaserScheme, in the rotating frame of its RotatingFrame,

\[\dot ρ = -i [H(t), ρ] + \sum_k \left( L_k ρ L_k^† - \tfrac{1}{2} \{ L_k^† L_k, ρ \} \right), \qquad H(t) = H_0 + \sum_j \left( M_j e^{-i w_j t} + M_j^† e^{+i w_j t} \right),\]

over the states of basis (the structure's basis, in its field eigenbasis): hamiltonian is the static $H_0$ (Hermitian, rad µs⁻¹) — the frame diagonal plus the couplings $(C + C^†)/2$ of all time-independent beam components, with C from coupling_matrix — and harmonics the $(w_j, M_j)$ pairs of the components left with a beat note (empty for a static model). couplings keeps the complex coupling matrix $C_b$ of each beam (rad µs⁻¹, upper⟨row|lower⟩⟨col|, exact at the static field) as it entered the Hamiltonian, beat-note components included — the per-beam split that the motional layer needs, e.g. to attach each beam's displacement operator in motional_model. jump_operators (µs⁻¹ᐟ²) are labelled by jump_labels (DecayLabel, DephasingLabel).

For a hyperfine species the basis states denote the adiabatically-labelled eigenstates at the static field, with exact at-field amplitudes throughout. Constructed via lindblad_model; structure and scheme are kept for the motional layer.

Coupling to motion

Mode geometry and recoil

Levels.OpticalBloch.MotionalMode — Type

One motional mode of the trapped ion: its (angular) frequency, unit direction in the frame with the quantisation axis along ẑ, and participation.

participation $b$ is the magnitude of the modelled ion's component in the mass-weighted normal-mode vector of a crystal, so that its position along direction is $b\sqrt{ħ/2Mω}(a + a^†)$ with $M$ the ion's own mass. It scales every Lamb–Dicke factor of the mode, beams and recoil alike; 1 is a single ion. A sign of the mode vector at this ion belongs in direction.

Constructed via MotionalMode(frequency, direction; participation = 1); the direction is normalised.

Levels.OpticalBloch.motional_coupling — Function
motional_coupling(model::LindbladModel, mode; recoil_moment = :exact)
motional_coupling(model::LindbladModel, modes::AbstractVector) -> Vector{MotionalCoupling}

Derives the MotionalCoupling of one MotionalMode (or of each of several) to the internal dynamics of model (whose structure supplies the species and whose scheme the beams): the projected Lamb–Dicke factor of each beam, the sideband Hamiltonian $\sum_l η_l \, i (C_l - C_l^†)/2$ (its beat-note harmonics kept alongside), and the recoil operators $\sqrt{α} η_0 L$ of the spontaneous-emission jump operators. recoil_moment = :exact evaluates $α$ per emitted component from the radiation patterns (recoil_moment), :isotropic takes uniform emission ($α = 1/3$), and a number uses that constant for every decay (e.g. the often-quoted $2/5$) — in which case only this first-order model is available, cf. motional_model.

source
Levels.OpticalBloch.MotionalCoupling — Type

Coupling of one MotionalMode to the internal dynamics of a LindbladModel, to first order in the Lamb–Dicke parameters.

With $x = x_0 (a + a^†)$, $x_0 = \sqrt{ħ / 2 M ω_m}$, along the mode direction, the full Hamiltonian is $H_\mathrm{int} ⊗ 1 + ω_m a^† a + H_\mathrm{sb} ⊗ (a + a^†)$ and each spontaneous-emission jump operator $L$ acquires the companion $\sqrt{α} η_0 L ⊗ (a + a^†)$ for the recoil of the emitted photon.

Two distinct Lamb–Dicke quantities appear, and the field names spell out which:

  • projected_lamb_dicke[l] $= (\vec{k}_l ⋅ \vec{e}_m) x_0$ is the signed, projected Lamb–Dicke factor of beam $l$ — the coefficient of $(a + a^†)$ in $e^{i \vec{k}_l ⋅ \vec{r}}$ — which is what enters the sideband Hamiltonian $H_\mathrm{sb} = \sum_l η_l \, i (C_l - C_l^†)/2$. For a Raman process through two beams, the difference of their values is the two-photon factor.
  • recoil_lamb_dicke[k] $= |\vec{k}| x_0$ is the unprojected Lamb–Dicke parameter of the decay transition behind jump operator $k$; the direction of the emitted photon is averaged over its radiation pattern into the second moment $α_k = ⟨(\hat{k} ⋅ \vec{e}_m)^2⟩$ (recoil_moments), which depends on the emitted spherical component $q$ and the angle between the mode and the quantisation axis: $1/5$ ($q = 0$) and $2/5$ ($|q| = 1$) for a mode along ẑ, $1/3$ on average over $q$ (cf. recoil_moment).

recoil_operators[k] is $\sqrt{α_k} η_{0,k} L_k$ for the $k$-th spontaneous-emission operator of the model (recoil_indices[k] its index in model.jump_operators; laser-dephasing operators carry no recoil). emission records how the moments were obtained — :exact from the radiation pattern of each emitted component, :isotropic for uniform emission, :constant for a user-supplied number — which is what decides whether the full emission direction distribution is available to motional_model beyond first order in the Lamb–Dicke parameters. sideband_harmonics mirrors model.harmonics for beams with a beat note.

Constructed via motional_coupling.

Levels.OpticalBloch.lamb_dicke — Function
lamb_dicke(species, mode::MotionalMode, wavevector; projected = true)
lamb_dicke(species, mode::MotionalMode, beam::LaserBeam; projected = true)

Returns the Lamb–Dicke factor of a plane wave with the given wavevector (a Cartesian 3-vector of inverse lengths, or the beam's $k = ω n / c$) and the given mode of an ion of the species' mass: the signed projected value $(\vec{k} ⋅ \vec{e}_m) \sqrt{ħ / 2 M ω_m}$ by default, or with projected = false the unprojected $|\vec{k}| \sqrt{ħ / 2 M ω_m}$, the conventional Lamb–Dicke parameter of the transition (cf. MotionalCoupling for which is which). Both are multiplied by the mode's participation.

source
Levels.OpticalBloch.recoil_moment — Function
recoil_moment(rank, q, direction)

Returns the second moment $α = ⟨(\hat{k} ⋅ \vec{e}_m)^2⟩$ of the direction of a photon emitted in the spherical component q of an electric multipole transition of the given rank, projected onto the unit mode direction (quantisation axis along ẑ): the fraction of the squared recoil that a spontaneous emission imparts, on average, to that mode.

By the azimuthal symmetry of the radiation patterns, $α = ⟨\hat{k}_z^2⟩ \cos^2 θ_m + \tfrac{1}{2}(1 - ⟨\hat{k}_z^2⟩) \sin^2 θ_m$ with $θ_m$ the angle between mode and quantisation axis; for electric-dipole emission $⟨\hat{k}_z^2⟩ = 1/5$ ($q = 0$) and $2/5$ ($|q| = 1$), so a mode along ẑ sees $1/5$ and $2/5$, one transverse to it $2/5$ and $3/10$, and the average over the three components is the isotropic $1/3$ in every direction (cf. Javanainen & Stenholm, Appl. Phys. 21, 35 (1980)). rank = 0 denotes isotropic emission, with $α = 1/3$ for every direction. The full distribution of $\hat{k} ⋅ \vec{e}_m$ behind this moment is available as a quadrature rule from emission_rule.

Joint internal and motional model

The joint model and all three cooling methods require an internal model without beat harmonics.

Levels.OpticalBloch.motional_model — Function
motional_model(model::LindbladModel, mc::MotionalCoupling; num_fock, lamb_dicke_order = 1, heating_rate = 0, quadrature_order = nothing, time_unit = u"µs")
motional_model(model::LindbladModel, mcs::AbstractVector{<:MotionalCoupling}; num_fock, …) -> MotionalModel

Builds an internal ⊗ Fock MotionalModel, with num_fock states per mode (one number or one per mode). For the full displacement operators,

\[H = H_\mathrm{frame}⊗1 + ∑_m ω_m a_m^†a_m + \frac12∑_b(C_b⊗D_b+\mathrm{h.c.}), \qquad D_b = e^{i∑_m η_{b,m}(a_m+a_m^†)}.\]

$H_\mathrm{frame}$ is the diagonal part of model.hamiltonian, excluding its beam couplings. $C_b$ is each beam's Rabi-frequency matrix and $η_{b,m}$ its projected Lamb–Dicke factor. Spontaneous emission contributes $L⊗e^{-i\vec k⋅\vec r}$ averaged over its radiation pattern (Kulosa et al., New J. Phys. 25, 053008 (2023), Eqs. (6), (7), (A.3)).

lamb_dicke_order selects the displacement approximation:

  • 1 (default) keeps the linear sideband Hamiltonian. Each decay operator acts as $L⊗1$ with separate recoil operators $\sqrt{α_m}η_{0,m}L⊗(a_m+a_m^†)$. This model reproduces the recoil heating $α_mη_{0,m}^2$ per emission and needs only the second emission moment; it accepts a constant recoil_moment and drops cross-mode recoil correlations.
  • A finite integer $k ≥ 2$ truncates beam and recoil displacements at total order $k$. Recoil uses emission_rule with default quadrature_order = k + 1.
  • Inf uses the full displacement matrix elements of displacement_elements. Each decay is replaced by quadrature operators $\sqrt{w_j}L⊗D(-η_0\vec h_j)$. The default quadrature_order is 12. Several modes use joint projections of emission directions from a product rule on the sphere, retaining their recoil correlations.

Orders above 1 require emission distributions from recoil_moment = :exact or :isotropic. For one mode, quadrature_order is the number of emission nodes; for several modes it is the polar order of the spherical product rule. Check convergence in Fock size and quadrature order; for finite expansions, also check the Lamb–Dicke order.

heating_rate (one rate or one per mode) adds $\sqrt{\dot n}a_m$ and $\sqrt{\dot n}a_m^†$. Laser phase diffusion acts on internal states only. Requires a static internal model, without beat harmonics. Operators are sparse; Hamiltonians are stripped to inverse time_unit and jumps to inverse square-root time_unit.

Note

Defined when QuantumToolbox.jl is loaded.

Levels.OpticalBloch.mean_phonon_number — Function

Returns the mean phonon number of each mode (a number for one mode, a vector for several) from its reduced populations $p_n$, $n=0,…,N-1$, where $N$ is that mode's num_fock.

Cooling estimates and dynamics

cooling_metrics is the common entry point for the steady occupation and a relaxation time per mode. The methods attach different meanings to that time:

MethodMotional treatmentMeaning of τ_c
AdiabaticEliminationIndependent rate equationsExponential occupation relaxation
SaturatedRatesNearest-neighbour phonon rate equations with non-perturbative ratesNormalised transient area from a specified thermal initial state
SemiclassicalRatesClassical coherent-state trajectories of all modesLocal $1/W_\mathrm{SC}$ at a specified occupation
IntegratedTransientJoint internal ⊗ Fock model, with optional coherence restrictionNormalised transient area from a specified thermal initial state
LiouvillianSpectrumJoint internal ⊗ Fock model, with optional coherence restrictionTime of a selected eigenmode with appreciable phonon-number weight

Estimator comparison

Adiabatic elimination replaces the joint dynamics by nearest-neighbour phonon rate equations with cooling and heating rates $nA_-$ and $(n+1)A_+$. Without background heating, the mean occupation obeys

\[\dot{\bar n} = -W(\bar n - \bar n_∞), \qquad W = A_- - A_+, \qquad \bar n_∞ = \frac{A_+}{A_- - A_+},\]

so all three methods give the time constant $1/W$ in this regime (Eschner et al. (2003), Eqs. (6)–(8)).

The reduction requires Lamb–Dicke confinement and internal relaxation much faster than motional evolution. The sideband coupling must remain weak enough to avoid saturating the internal resonance it addresses. In EIT cooling, the narrow dressed-state linewidth can set this scale, so $ηΩ/ω_m$ alone is not a general validity test (Morigi (2003), discussion after Eq. (33) and comparison with quantum Monte Carlo in Fig. 3). Sideband matrix elements grow as $\sqrt{n}$, making the initial occupation relevant to validity. The Lamb–Dicke expansion and adiabatic elimination are separate approximations: a first-order joint model (lamb_dicke_order = 1) still retains coherent, saturated sideband dynamics.

Outside the rate-equation regime, cooling can be non-exponential and the motional state need not remain thermal. Scharnhorst et al. (2018) demonstrate this for EIT cooling: strong spin–motion coupling disrupts the dark state and slows the initial cooling (Sec. IV.B.2, Fig. 7). Their comparison of analytical rates, master-equation simulations and experiment illustrates the breakdown of the reduced theory (Sec. V.4, Fig. 13).

SaturatedRates keeps a rate equation for the phonon populations, but evaluates its rates without expanding in the sideband coupling, including the Franck–Condon factors of Morigi et al. (1999) at all orders in $η$. It recovers adiabatic elimination for weak sideband coupling and models saturation through a frozen-coefficient ladder calculation. It propagates a full phonon distribution without a closure for its moments, at the cost of a few internal-state solves per octave in $n$. The local rates match the ladder's drift and diffusion unless an empirical Fock-window closure or positivity clipping replaces the diffusion. Recoil includes the correlation between emission rate and motional phase. Neither local matching nor harmonic convergence guarantees the accuracy of the full distribution, mean trajectory or steady occupation; the cold and hot benchmarks cover selected regimes. PhononRateEquation describes the approximations and their limits.

The equation's diagnostics records each solved node before interpolation and added external heating. For example, filter(d -> d.source == :window || d.clipped, r.diagnostics) selects nodes whose window closure or positivity floor may change the calculated diffusion. Each Levels.OpticalBloch.RungRateDiagnostics also contains the raw cumulants, selected total rate and the status of any attempted window ratio, including rejected ratios.

semiclassical_cooling_rates goes further from the quantum motion: it replaces it by a classical trajectory at fixed energy and averages the laser power (Bartolotta et al. (2024), "PACMAN"). This gives only the drift $W_\mathrm{SC}(\vec n)$, without recoil, $A_+$ or a steady occupation, but the rate of each mode then depends on the energy in all others, which the independent modes of the rate equations cannot capture, at a cost independent of the Fock ladder. The two meet for one mode: the net rate of a PhononRateEquation rung tends to the semiclassical drift as $n$ grows, given a bandwidth of at least $2 \max_b |η_b| \sqrt{n}$ harmonics.

The two joint-model estimates describe different aspects of this relaxation. For a diagonalisable generator, a decaying occupation transient has the form

\[\bar n(t) - \bar n_∞ = ∑_j a_j e^{λ_j t}, \qquad τ_\mathrm{int} = \frac{∫_0^∞ [\bar n(t) - \bar n_∞]\,\mathrm{d}t}{\bar n(0) - \bar n_∞} = -\frac{∑_j a_j/λ_j}{∑_j a_j},\]

where the sum runs over decaying modes and the coefficients $a_j$ depend on both the initial state and the phonon-number operator. IntegratedTransient returns $τ_\mathrm{int}$, an effective cooling time for that preparation. This avoids choosing a fit window for an exponential, but retains the dependence on initial occupation (Kulosa et al. (2023), Eqs. (10)–(14), Fig. 2(c) and Fig. C1). LiouvillianSpectrum returns $-1/\mathrm{Re}\,λ$ for a selected mode with appreciable phonon-number weight. The selection is independent of the preparation; the mode's contribution to an actual transient is not. A weak tail can set the late-time exponent while contributing little to the integral. Since the $a_j$ can be negative or complex, the integral is not generally an average of modal times with positive weights.

Choosing a method

  • Use AdiabaticElimination for parameter scans of unsaturated cooling (it is also accurate in Doppler cooling, where the rates per quantum hardly vary along the occupied part of the ladder), then check promising points with a joint model. Compare both occupation and time; agreement at weak drive does not establish accuracy near a saturated optimum.
  • Use SaturatedRates as the objective for optimising cooling from a hot state, or wherever $ηΩ\sqrt{n}$ may reach the relevant internal linewidth, with time_to_nbar for a target occupation. Verify the result with a joint model; the rate equation assumes independent modes.
  • Use semiclassical_cooling_rates and thermal_cooling_rates for the competition between many hot modes (a Doppler-cooled crystal), or for a capture range at occupations beyond a Fock ladder. It says nothing about the final occupation.
  • Use LiouvillianSpectrum for late-time occupation relaxation. Check the selected mode as num_fock, num_eigenpairs and weight_threshold change. If the time keeps increasing with Fock size, it has not converged; evaluate cooling from the relevant preparation with IntegratedTransient or cooling_curve.
  • Use IntegratedTransient for an effective cooling time from a specified initial occupation, especially a hot state. Set initial_nbar to that preparation and allow fock_truncation to size the ladder. Use cooling_curve to verify the time to reach a target occupation number or the shape of the transient.

For either joint method, check convergence in Fock size, coherence bandwidth and Lamb–Dicke order. A narrow bandwidth can greatly reduce memory and factorisation cost; increase it until the observables stabilise. For a hot state, use cooling_curve with fock = true to inspect the upper tail during cooling. A first-order model can make a large ladder affordable, but its accuracy must be checked over the occupied states. When comparing the spectral and integral times, use the same model settings: their defaults differ in both lamb_dicke_order and bandwidth.

Adiabatic elimination

Levels.OpticalBloch.AdiabaticElimination — Type
AdiabaticElimination()

Selects the order-$η^2$ rate equations for cooling_metrics, obtained by adiabatically eliminating the internal dynamics. Requires Lamb–Dicke motion and weak sideband coupling, with internal relaxation fast compared with the induced motional rates. For saturated sidebands, see SaturatedRates or a full motional model.

\[A_± = 2 \mathrm{Re} S(±ω_m) + D, \qquad S(ω) = \mathrm{Tr}\left[H_\mathrm{sb}(-\mathcal L+iω)^{-1} (H_\mathrm{sb}ρ_∞)\right],\]

where $\mathcal L$ is the internal Liouvillian, $ρ_∞$ its steady state and $H_\mathrm{sb}$ the coefficient of $a+a^†$ in the Hamiltonian (angular frequency units). Spontaneous-emission recoil adds the same diffusion term to both rates, $D = ∑_k α_k η_{0,k}^2 \mathrm{Tr}(L_k^†L_kρ_∞)$, with the decay operators, recoil factors and emission moments of MotionalCoupling. See Cirac et al., Phys. Rev. A 46, 2668 (1992), and Morigi, Phys. Rev. A 67, 033402 (2003).

The resolvent uses the $e^{-iωt}$ Fourier convention, so cooling samples $S(-ω_m)$. In the weak-excitation two-level limit, $A_∓-D ≃ η^2 Γ|Ω|^2/[Γ^2+4(δ±ω_m)^2]$ for decay rate $Γ$, Rabi frequency $Ω$, projected Lamb–Dicke factor $η$ and laser-minus-atomic detuning $δ$: a red sideband at $δ=-ω_m$ enhances $A_-$.

$A_+$ raises and $A_-$ lowers the phonon number:

\[\dot{\bar n} = A_+(\bar n+1)-A_-\bar n+\dot n_\mathrm{heat}, \qquad \bar n_∞ = \frac{A_++\dot n_\mathrm{heat}}{A_--A_+}, \qquad τ_c = \frac{1}{A_--A_+}.\]

The CoolingMetrics fields nbar and τ_c are NaN when $A_- ≤ A_+$. Several modes are treated independently, sharing the internal steady state and Liouvillian. The internal model must have no beat harmonics.

Levels.OpticalBloch.PhononRateEquation — Type
PhononRateEquation(model::LindbladModel, mc::MotionalCoupling; num_fock, lamb_dicke_order = Inf, bandwidth = 2, node_ratio = 2, heating_rate = 0u"s^-1", time_unit = u"µs")
PhononRateEquation(lowering_rates, raising_rates, time_unit)

The nearest-neighbour rate equations $\dot p_n = Γ^↑_n p_{n-1} + Γ^↓_{n+1} p_{n+1} - (Γ^↓_n + Γ^↑_{n+1}) p_n$ of the phonon populations of one mode, with lowering_rates $Γ^↓_n$ ($n → n - 1$) and raising_rates $Γ^↑_n$ ($n - 1 → n$) for $n = 1 … \mathtt{num\_fock} - 1$. Unlike AdiabaticElimination, whose rates $nA_∓$ are second order in the sideband coupling, these saturate once $ηΩ\sqrt{n}$ reaches the internal relaxation rate.

Fock-resolved rate equations are the low-saturation result of Morigi et al., Phys. Rev. A 59, 3797 (1999), Eq. (15), and saturated sideband transfer in a two-state model that of Phatak et al., Phys. Rev. A 110, 043116 (2024), Eqs. (66)–(68). Here, each rung is solved for arbitrary internal structure and drive, from the steady state of a ladder with the couplings of rung $n$ on every rung: a Floquet problem with bandwidth harmonics, approaching the classical power absorbed from an oscillation of amplitude $∝\sqrt{n}$ at large occupation. The bare population of a Fock state would not do: on a saturated sideband, it is coherently shared with its neighbour. The net rate $Γ^↑_n - Γ^↓_n$ is the mean phonon-number current of the ladder. By default the total $Γ^↑_n + Γ^↓_n$ matches its diffusion, the second counting-cumulant rate, including emission recoil at frozen amplitude with its motional-phase dependence. The closure instead attempts a ratio $Γ^↑_n/Γ^↓_n$ from the closed internal ⊗ Fock window $n-2 … n+1$ when $B ≤ 10|D|$ and the sideband reach $2|η|\sqrt{n} ≤ 1$, or whenever $B < |D|$. Here $D$ and $B$ are the ladder's drift and diffusion; the reach includes two-photon differences of the beams' projected Lamb–Dicke factors. These switches are empirical. The window avoids subtracting nearly equal numbers to infer a small rate, but has no general convergence guarantee. It is used only when its ratio and the drift give positive matching rates. A positivity floor enforces $Γ^↑_n + Γ^↓_n ≥ (1 + 10^{-9})|D|$; window matching or clipping generally abandons the ladder's diffusion.

For weak sidebands, the rates are $Γ^↓_n = nA_-$ and $Γ^↑_n = nA_+$. lamb_dicke_order = Inf uses the full displacement elements of displacement_elements in the couplings (the carrier averaged over the rung) and the emission rule of emission_rule in the recoil; 1 follows the first-order motional_model and also accepts a constant recoil_moment. Rungs are solved at nodes at most node_ratio apart, refined around zeros of the carrier and first-sideband elements (dark Fock states), and interpolated between (the net rate per quantum linearly in $\ln n$, the slower rate per quantum in logs); node_ratio = 1 solves every rung. heating_rate adds $n \dot n_\mathrm{heat}$ to both rates.

diagnostics contains a RungRateDiagnostics for each solved node, before interpolation and added external heating. It exposes the raw drift and diffusion, selected total, closure source, attempted window ratio and positivity clipping. The explicit-rate constructor has empty diagnostics.

As independent steps between neighbouring Fock states, this cannot represent sub-Poissonian statistics or the bypassing of a dark Fock state through a second sideband, next to which the ladder can understate the diffusion. Both closures can be inaccurate around a reach of one. Errors can affect the spread, mean trajectory and steady occupation. In particular, the conditional drift at state $n$ is $Γ^↑_{n+1} - Γ^↓_n$, not the matched bond drift $D_n$. Modes are independent: their competition for the same scattering events is not captured. Requires an internal model without beat harmonics. A heated mode accumulates at the truncation, where steady_value depends on num_fock.

steady_value, fock_populations, integral_relaxation_time, cooling_curve and time_to_nbar evaluate the equation.

Levels.OpticalBloch.semiclassical_phonon_rates — Function
semiclassical_phonon_rates(model::LindbladModel, mcs::AbstractVector{<:MotionalCoupling}, occupations; engine = nothing, rng = default_rng(), time_unit = u"µs")

Returns the mean rates of change $R_μ = ⟨\dot n_μ⟩$ (inverse time_unit) of the occupations of the given coherent-state motion: $⟨\hat a_μ⟩ = \sqrt{n_μ} e^{-i(ω_μ t + θ_μ)}$, held at fixed energy, with the secular phases $θ_μ$ averaged out. This is the semiclassical method of Bartolotta et al., arXiv:2411.18818 (PACMAN). Replacing the motion by its expectation value turns each beam's displacement operator into the phase $φ_b(t) = ∑_μ 2η_{bμ}\sqrt{n_μ} \cos(ω_μ t + θ_μ)$, and a transformation with the beams' traversals of the rotating-frame tree moves those phases into Doppler shifts,

\[H(t) = H - ∑_μ 2\sqrt{n_μ} ω_μ \sin(ω_μ t + θ_μ) K_μ, \qquad K_μ = \mathrm{diag}\Big(∑_b n_{i,b} η_{bμ}\Big), \qquad R_μ = \Big⟨2\sqrt{n_μ} \sin(ω_μ t + θ_μ) \mathrm{Tr}[H_{\mathrm{sb},μ} ρ(t)]\Big⟩,\]

with H the model's static Hamiltonian and $H_{\mathrm{sb},μ}$ the mode's sideband_hamiltonian.

The expansion in $η$ and the separation of timescales are both avoided, and the modes are coupled through their common Doppler signal, so each $R_μ$ depends on the energy in all modes. Conversely, there is no recoil and no heating from the blue sideband ($A_+$), hence no steady occupation: the rates are the drift of a hot mode, reliable for $η\sqrt{2n+1} ≳ 0.1$, and in the limit $n → 0$ return $-n (A_- - A_+)$ of AdiabaticElimination.

engine defaults to HarmonicBalance for at most one non-zero occupation and to TimeAverage, with random phases from rng, otherwise. Requires a static model. Rates of modes with zero occupation are zero.

Liouvillian spectrum

Levels.OpticalBloch.LiouvillianSpectrum — Type
LiouvillianSpectrum(; num_fock, lamb_dicke_order = 1, bandwidth = 2, num_eigenpairs = 12, dense_limit = 200, weight_threshold = 0.1)

Selects the stationary occupation and a spectral relaxation time of a motional_model for cooling_metrics. num_fock and lamb_dicke_order set the model's truncation; BandLiouvillian retains only coherences within bandwidth in each mode (nothing retains all). The slow modes live close to the diagonal: a band of 2 reproduces the full generator's mode and occupation to five digits in the sideband- and dark-resonance-cooling models tested, from the Lamb–Dicke regime to saturation, at a factorisation cost 20–250 times lower. relaxation_modes supplies the num_eigenpairs nonzero eigenmodes nearest $λ=0$, ordered by increasing $|λ|$.

For mode $m$, the time is $τ_c=-1/\mathrm{Re}\,λ$ of the first eigenmode whose phonon-number weight $w_m(v)=|\mathrm{Tr}(\hat N_m v)|/‖v‖_\mathrm{F}$ is at least weight_threshold times the largest weight in the computed set. Here $v$ is the reshaped eigenvector and $‖⋅‖_\mathrm{F}$ the Frobenius norm. The threshold suppresses motional coherences with little phonon-number weight; in the rate-equation limit these can decay at half the occupation rate.

The selected time is independent of the initial state; an actual transient also depends on which modes that state excites. Thresholding can omit weak tails, and ordering by $|λ|$ need not order decay rates. Convergence checks include num_eigenpairs, threshold, Fock size, bandwidth and displacement order. A time that keeps growing with num_fock means the slowest weighted mode sits at the truncation — the rate per quantum falls along the ladder, as in saturated dark-resonance cooling — and no asymptotic rate exists; IntegratedTransient from the relevant initial state applies instead. dense_limit sets the dense-diagonalisation cutoff of relaxation_modes. An error is raised if no mode qualifies; a negative time signals a growing mode.

IntegratedTransient measures the area of a specified transient.

Returns a quadrature rule — nodes h and weights w with $\sum_j w_j = 1$ — for the distribution of the projection $h = \hat{k} ⋅ \vec{e}_m$ of the direction of a photon emitted in the spherical component q of an electric multipole transition of the given rank (rank = 0: isotropic emission) onto the unit mode direction, quantisation axis along ẑ: $∫ K(h) f(h) \, dh ≈ \sum_j w_j f(h_j)$. Phys. 25, 053008 (2023), Eq. (A.3)).

For one direction the result is the Gauss rule of the measure $K(h) \, dh$ with quadrature_order points, exact for polynomials of degree $2 \, \mathtt{quadrature_order} - 1$, obtained by the Stieltjes procedure (Lanczos with full reorthogonalisation) and the Golub–Welsch eigenvalue method on a fine product quadrature of the sphere (Gautschi, Orthogonal Polynomials: Computation and Approximation, Oxford (2004), Secs. 2.2 and 3.1). For several directions the nodes are the joint projections of a product rule on the sphere — row $j$ of the matrix h holds $\hat{k}_j ⋅ \vec{e}_m$ for every direction $m$ — with quadrature_order Gauss–Legendre points in $\cos θ$ and twice as many equidistant azimuths, exact for integrands polynomial of degree up to $2 \, \mathtt{quadrature_order} - 5$ in the direction cosines (the pattern itself being of degree four).

Liouvillian construction and representation

Levels.OpticalBloch.BandLiouvillian — Type
BandLiouvillian(H, c_ops, num_states, num_fock; bandwidth = nothing, time_unit = u"µs")
BandLiouvillian(mm::MotionalModel; bandwidth = nothing)       (extension)
BandLiouvillian(model::LindbladModel; time_unit = u"µs")      (extension)

The Lindblad generator on internal ⊗ Fock space, restricted to density-matrix elements $ρ_{(i\vec n),(j\vec m)}$ with $|n_ℓ-m_ℓ|≤\mathtt{bandwidth}$ for every mode $ℓ$. bandwidth = nothing retains all coherences; num_fock = Int[] gives an internal-only model.

Plain H and c_ops matrices use an internal factor of num_states, followed by Fock factors of sizes num_fock (last mode fastest). Their entries use inverse time_unit and inverse square-root time_unit, respectively. The model constructors supply these matrices and their time scale.

For bandwidth $K$ small compared with the Fock sizes, the generator's dimension falls from $(N_\mathrm{int}∏_ℓN_ℓ)^2$ to about $N_\mathrm{int}^2∏_ℓ[(2K+1)N_ℓ]$. This is a projection of the Liouvillian; increase bandwidth and Fock sizes to check convergence.

L stores the sparse generator. entries maps band indices to density-matrix indices (r, c); index maps back, with zero outside the band. band_vector and full_matrix convert representations. cooling_curve propagates the equation, IntegratedTransientSolver finds steady states and integral relaxation times, and relaxation_modes the slowest eigenmodes.

Steady states and transient integrals

IntegratedTransientSolver supports arbitrary observables and initial states, including internal-state problems without Fock factors.

Levels.OpticalBloch.IntegratedTransientSolver — Type
IntegratedTransientSolver(bl::BandLiouvillian, observables; atol = 1e-9)
IntegratedTransientSolver(mm::MotionalModel; bandwidth = nothing, observables)   (extension)
IntegratedTransientSolver(model::LindbladModel, observables; time_unit)       (extension)

Solves for a unique steady state and integrated transients of a relaxing Liouvillian. The transient integral satisfies

\[\hat τ = ∫_0^∞[ρ(t)-ρ_∞]\,dt, \qquad \mathcal L\hat τ = ρ_∞-ρ_0, \qquad \mathrm{Tr}\hat τ=0.\]

For an observable $A$, the integral relaxation time is $τ_A = \mathrm{Tr}(A\hat τ)/[\mathrm{Tr}(Aρ_0)-\mathrm{Tr}(Aρ_∞)]$. This is the τ-matrix method of Il'enkov et al., JETP 123, 1 (2016), and Kulosa et al., New J. Phys. 25, 053008 (2023), Sec. 3, Eqs. (10)–(14); see also integral_relaxation_time.

One sparse LU factorisation replaces a population row by the trace constraint. A forward solve gives the steady state; a transposed solve per observable stores the linear functional needed for its transient integral. Later initial states require only dot products. observables is one matrix or a vector of matrices; the motional-model form defaults to phonon numbers.

Strict partial pivoting and iterative refinement handle poorly scaled Fock ladders. Solves are checked by normwise backward error; steady populations and trace are checked to atol. The factorisation is released after use.

Relaxation eigenmodes

Levels.OpticalBloch.relaxation_modes — Function
relaxation_modes(bl::BandLiouvillian; num_eigenpairs = 12, dense_limit = 200) -> (; values, vectors, steady)

Returns the num_eigenpairs eigenmodes nearest the Liouvillian's origin: its nonzero eigenvalues $λ$ of smallest magnitude, in order of increasing magnitude, as complex rates in inverse time_unit ($-\mathrm{Re}\,λ$ is the decay rate, $\mathrm{Im}\,λ$ the oscillation frequency; a positive real part marks a spurious growing mode of a truncated model), with the corresponding eigenvectors as unit-norm band-vector columns and the stationary state as a unit-trace band vector. The modes are traceless: they describe how an observable's deviation from its steady value decays. Ordering by $|λ|$ need not order the decay rates $-\mathrm{Re}\,λ$ when the modes oscillate.

Generators of dimension up to dense_limit are diagonalised densely; the Krylov path below is faster beyond a few hundred. Larger ones are treated by Krylov–Schur iteration (ArnoldiMethod.jl) on the group inverse of $\mathcal L$ — its inverse on the traceless subspace, applied through the trace-row factorisation of IntegratedTransientSolver — whose dominant eigenvalues are the reciprocals of the slowest nonzero rates. Unlike shift-and-invert at zero, this excludes the stationary state exactly instead of letting its numerically singular image swamp the Krylov space, and it needs no shift on the scale of the unknown relaxation rate. The iteration starts from a fixed vector and is deterministic, converging the reciprocal rates to a relative residual of $10^{-12}$. Eigenvalue errors can be amplified by their condition numbers. A unique steady state is required.
