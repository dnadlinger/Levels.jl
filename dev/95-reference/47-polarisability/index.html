Light shifts and polarisabilities

The light-shift model combines an electric-dipole (E1) background with near-resonant channels resolved into individual Zeeman components. The background uses explicit dipole strengths plus a static scalar/tensor remainder; the channels use Einstein A coefficients and at-field couplings. E1 shifts depend on polarisation. Electric-quadrupole (E2) channels also depend on the beam direction.

State polarisability

polarisability returns the E1 response of a state in SI units ($C m^2/V$). With no polarisation argument, it returns three entries for pure $σ^-$, $π$ and $σ^+$ fields, indexed by $q + 2$. A polarisation argument contracts these with the normalised weights $w_q = |ε_{-q}|^2$. The $π$ entry describes a field along the quantisation axis; the mean of the two $σ$ entries describes a linear field perpendicular to it.

A wavelength or angular frequency selects the dynamic response; 0u"rad/s" selects the static response. For example, the static differential polarisability sets the electric Stark shift from micromotion in a Paul trap:

using Levels, Unitful

s = StateSpec("S_1/2 F=4", 4)
d = StateSpec("D_5/2 F=4", 3)
B0 = insensitive_field(ca43, s => d, (0.45u"mT", 0.55u"mT"))
atom = Eigenstructure(ca43, "S_1/2", "D_5/2"; B=B0)
Δα = polarisability(atom, d, 0u"rad/s") - polarisability(atom, s, 0u"rad/s")
Δα_parallel = Δα[2]
Δα_perpendicular = (Δα[1] + Δα[3]) / 2
θ = π / 4
Δα_tilted = cos(θ)^2 * Δα_parallel + sin(θ)^2 * Δα_perpendicular
(; Δα_parallel, Δα_perpendicular, Δα_tilted)
(Δα_parallel = -6.558594791951873e-40 C m^2 V^-1, Δα_perpendicular = -7.610019910728578e-40 C m^2 V^-1, Δα_tilted = -7.084307351340225e-40 C m^2 V^-1)

For a sinusoidal electric field with unit polarisation and peak amplitude $E_0$, the cycle-averaged energy shift is $-α E_0^2/4$. For a static field it is $-α E^2/2$. For a light beam of intensity $I$, the angular-frequency shift is $-I α/(2c ε_0 ħ)$.

The species form evaluates zero-field $|J,m_J⟩$ or $|F,m_F⟩$ states. The structure form evaluates the adiabatically labelled eigenstates at the field of the Eigenstructure, including $F$ mixing; fine-structure eigenvectors remain unchanged, but their Zeeman energies enter the denominators. The scalar/vector/tensor level accessors describe zero-field levels; their single-$F$ angular factors do not apply to mixed field eigenstates. The result is the diagonal second-order response of a labelled eigenstate: within a degenerate subspace the effective light-shift operator may mix the states, and its eigenvalues are then the observed shifts.

Parked and driven beams

For a parked beam, light_shift returns the angular-frequency shift of one state. The shift of a transition in a Ramsey measurement is the upper state's shift minus the lower state's shift.

A nearby laser is specified by RelativeFrequency, an angular offset from a named zero-field interval. The uncertain tabulated line centre then cancels from the component detunings, leaving the offset and the Zeeman/hyperfine splittings. See Laser frequencies.

laser = RelativeFrequency(s.level => d.level, angular(20u"MHz"))
c = LightShiftCoefficients(atom, laser)
intensity = 1u"W/m^2"
n, ε = beam_vectors(π / 2, π / 4, 0.3)
Δω_parked = light_shift(c, d, intensity, ε; direction=n) -
            light_shift(c, s, intensity, ε; direction=n)
3.5163874217732144e-9 rad μs^-1

For a resonantly driven component, driven_light_shift returns the displacement of its resonance. It evaluates spectator channels with the laser tuned to the probed component and excludes that component as the coherent drive. A positive result raises the resonance frequency.

Δω_driven = driven_light_shift(c, s => d, intensity, ε; direction=n)
3.9714063278485616e-9 rad μs^-1

Both APIs accept parts = :total | :background | :resonant (default :total). The resonant part uses Einstein A coefficients and field-resolved couplings. E2 channels are built for pairs among the structure's manifolds; E1 channels require that pair to be the RelativeFrequency reference. Channels coupled at exactly zero detuning raise an error; a closed cycle without spectators can be evaluated at zero field.

Coefficients cache the atomic structure for repeated intensity, polarisation and detuning evaluations. A parked evaluation's δ keyword changes only the near-resonant detunings; the smooth background stays at the construction frequency. A driven evaluation that includes the background requires a construction frequency within relative tolerance $10^{-3}$ of the probed line.

Background and channel split

For an E1 reference pair, the near-resonant term moves from the background into the channel sum. The counter-rotating term stays in the background, so the split counts every term once at the construction frequency. The reference channel must be explicit in each available LevelPolarisability background; a lumped remainder cannot be split. If a level has no background data, its channels remain usable with parts = :resonant.

Bare frequencies within ~100 GHz of an explicit E1 line are refused: precise component detunings require a reference that cancels the uncertain line centre. The state polarisability accessor has the same guard and rejects RelativeFrequency; the level scalar/vector/tensor accessors remain unguarded curves.

For hyperfine states, explicit E1 channels resolve the known hyperfine splittings of both levels. This can produce a tensor response even for an $F$ level of a $J = 1/2$ parent. At a field, amplitudes rotate into both field eigenbases before squaring, and denominators use their eigen-energies. The static remainder is an electronic operator evaluated in the source eigenstate, retaining coherences between its $F$ components.

An intermediate level without hyperfine constants is approximated as degenerate at its centroid, with no Zeeman shift. Field-resolved source levels and near-resonant partners require known constants.

Validity and symmetry

The background requires polarisability data: S$_{1/2}$ and D$_{5/2}$ for sr88, and also D$_{3/2}$ for ca40 and ca43. Evaluating a background for a level without data raises an error; its near-resonant channels can still be used with parts = :resonant. The static remainder approximates unresolved E1 channels and is suitable only far from their resonances. E2 contributions outside the selected near-resonant pair and counter-rotating E2 terms are omitted.

The channel sum uses second-order perturbation theory with unregularised $1/detuning$ poles. Evaluation warns if a channel's Rabi frequency exceeds one tenth of its detuning or the laser lies within five upper-level linewidths of a pole. Linewidths enter these warnings, not the denominators; the model provides no scattering rate.

For fine-structure E2 transitions, spectator detunings scale with B, so the resonant shift scales as $I/B$. With linear polarisation, reversing both magnetic projections changes this part's sign; it cancels in a $±m$ pair average ([Lindvall2025], Sec. III F 2). Ellipticity breaks that cancellation. The E1 background generally remains in the pair average.

Hyperfine spectators in other $F$ levels generally prevent the resonant pair cancellation and the $I/B$ scaling. For linear polarisation, the field-reversal identity is $shift(-m,B) = shift(+m,-B)$, with both projections reversed; for elliptical polarisation the field reversal also requires conjugating ε.

Levels.BACKGROUND_GUARD_WINDOW — Constant

A bare laser frequency closer than this to an explicit electric-dipole channel requires a RelativeFrequency reference: its channel detunings must cancel the uncertain tabulated line centre.

Levels.LightShiftCoefficients — Method
LightShiftCoefficients(s::Eigenstructure, laser)

Precomputes light-shift data for every state in basis(s), for a laser given as a wavelength, angular frequency or RelativeFrequency. Reuses the structure's field eigenstates for repeated intensity, polarisation and detuning evaluations.

The E1 background uses LevelPolarisability data; rows without data are stored as NaN and raise an error if evaluated. Near-resonant channels use Einstein A coefficients and are built for E2 pairs among the structure's manifolds and for the RelativeFrequency pair (E1 or E2). Neither part includes magnetic-dipole (M1) couplings to the beam's magnetic field; an M1 reference pair raises an error.

For an E1 reference pair, its co-rotating term is moved from each available background into the channel sum; the partner must be explicit in that background's dipole data. A bare frequency within ~100 GHz of an explicit E1 line is refused; use RelativeFrequency to cancel the tabulated line centre from precise detunings.

light_shift evaluates a parked beam and driven_light_shift a resonantly driven component. See Light shifts and polarisabilities for the background/channel split and validity limits.

Levels.ResonantChannel — Type

One near-resonant coupling channel of a basis state: a Zeeman component connecting it to partner in the other manifold of a level pair the laser sits close to.

The signed weight is such that the second-order shift of the state from this channel is weight × intensity × w / (δ − Δ), with w the geometric weight of its $Δm = q$ component (polarisation_weights[q + 2] for rank 1, quadrupole_weights[q + 3] for rank 2) and δ the laser offset from the same reference Δ is measured against. It is positive for a state in the lower manifold of the pair and negative for one in the upper (level repulsion away from the driving photon); 4 × |weight| × intensity × w is the squared Rabi frequency of the component, and γ the total decay rate of the upper level — both used by the validity warnings at evaluation time.

Levels.channel_scalar_polarisability — Method

Returns one E1 channel's scalar polarisability at photon energy ħω: $2Δ d^2 / [3(2j+1)(Δ^2-(ħω)^2)]$, for source angular momentum j, reduced dipole d and signed intermediate-level energy difference Δ.

Also supplies the static contribution subtracted when resolving an ImplicitPolarisability, keeping the anchor and evaluation consistent.

Levels.channel_tensor_polarisability — Method

Returns one E1 channel's tensor polarisability for source angular momentum j > 1/2 and intermediate angular momentum j_channel. Other arguments are as for Levels.channel_scalar_polarisability.

Subtracts $α_0$ from the stretched state's π-polarised response $α_0 + α_2$.

Levels.driven_light_shift — Method
driven_light_shift(coefficients::LightShiftCoefficients, transition::Pair, intensity, ε; direction, parts)
driven_light_shift(s::Eigenstructure, lower::StateSpec => upper::StateSpec, intensity, ε; direction, parts)

Returns the angular-frequency displacement of the lower => upper resonance under a laser tuned to that component. A positive result raises the resonance frequency; both states must be in the structure's selected basis.

parts selects :total (default), :background or :resonant. The background is the difference of the states' E1 shifts. The resonant part sums spectator channels sharing either state, with detunings relative to the probed component. The probed channel itself is excluded as the coherent drive. For E2 this is the ac Stark shift of [Lindvall2025], Sec. III F 2; its symmetries are described in Light shifts and polarisabilities.

An E2 pair needs channels in the coefficients and a beam direction. An E1 pair must be the RelativeFrequency reference pair; direction is unused. Evaluating the background also requires its construction frequency to match the probed line within relative tolerance $10^{-3}$.

The spectator sum uses second-order perturbation theory, with the warnings of light_shift. A coupled spectator at the probed frequency raises an error; a closed cycle without spectators is valid at zero field. :background skips the channels and does not check drivability. The structure form constructs coefficients with the pair's zero-field interval as the reference on every call.

References

  • [Lindvall2025]: T. Lindvall, A. E. Wallin, K. J. Hanhijärvi, and T. Fordell, "⁸⁸Sr⁺ Optical Clock with 7.9 × 10⁻¹⁹ Systematic Uncertainty and Measurement of Its Absolute Frequency", Phys. Rev. Applied 24, 044082 (2025), doi:10.1103/cztf-bfvp.

Levels.light_shift — Method
light_shift(coefficients::LightShiftCoefficients, state::StateSpec, intensity, ε; direction, δ, parts)
light_shift(s::Eigenstructure, state::StateSpec, laser, intensity, ε; direction, δ, parts)

Returns the angular-frequency shift of state under a beam parked at the coefficients' laser frequency. The state must belong to the structure's selected basis. A parked transition shift is the upper state's shift minus the lower state's; driven_light_shift instead describes a resonantly driven component.

parts selects :total (default), :background or :resonant. The E1 background depends on polarisation ε. For a RelativeFrequency laser, the resonant sum adds channels to the other fine-structure manifold of the reference pair. direction is required for an E2 reference pair and unused for E1. With a bare frequency only the background is evaluated; requesting :resonant raises an error.

δ overrides the reference offset in the channel detunings. The background stays at the construction frequency; rebuild the coefficients when its variation matters. The structure form rebuilds them on every call.

The result is a diagonal second-order response, with the degeneracy caveat of polarisability. Channel couplings must be small compared with their detunings. Evaluation warns near strong coupling or linewidth-scale detunings; a coupled channel at exactly zero detuning raises an error.

Levels.manifold_polarisabilities — Method

Returns a length(source.basis) × 3 matrix of E1 polarisabilities of the eigenstates of the source manifold of s, indexed by [state, q + 2] like polarisation_weights. Each intermediate state contributes $|d_{ts}|^2 [w_q/(E_t-E_s-ħω) + w_{-q}/(E_t-E_s+ħω)]$, with dipole matrix element $d_{ts}$ and energies at the structure's field. Adds the static scalar remainder and the tensor remainder weighted by Levels.tensor_expectations.

exclude names an explicit intermediate level whose near-resonant term moves into LightShiftCoefficients's channel sum. The counter-rotating term stays here; a lumped remainder cannot be split.

Levels.polarisability — Method
polarisability(species, state::StateSpec, frequency[, ε])
polarisability(s::Eigenstructure, state::StateSpec, frequency[, ε])

Returns a state's E1 polarisability in SI units ($C m^2/V$). Without ε, returns a vector for pure $σ^-$, $π$ and $σ^+$ fields, indexed by q + 2. With ε, contracts it with polarisation_weights, ignoring the scale of ε. For a linear field, the $π$ entry applies along ẑ and the mean of the $σ$ entries applies perpendicular to it.

frequency accepts a wavelength or angular frequency; 0u"rad/s" gives the static response. Frequencies within ~100 GHz of an explicit E1 line are refused. For that regime use LightShiftCoefficients with a RelativeFrequency, which this accessor does not accept.

The species form uses zero-field states. The structure form accepts any label in coupled_basis(s) and uses its field eigenstate, including $F$ mixing. Explicit dipole channels resolve source and intermediate hyperfine and Zeeman energies. A hyperfine intermediate without constants is instead approximated as degenerate at its centroid, without Zeeman structure. A static scalar/tensor remainder accounts for unresolved channels.

The result is a diagonal second-order response, including co- and counter-rotating E1 terms. Within a degenerate subspace the effective light-shift operator can mix states; its eigenvalues then give the physical shifts, rather than these diagonal entries. For a sinusoidal field of peak amplitude $E$, the cycle-averaged energy shift is $-α E^2/4$; for a static field it is $-α E^2/2$. For light intensity $I$ the angular shift is $-I α/(2c ε_0 ħ)$.

Levels.polarisation_weights — Method
polarisation_weights(ε)

Returns the fractions $w_q = |ε_{-q}|^2$ of the beam intensity driving each of the $Δm = q$ electric-dipole channels, as a length-3 tuple indexed by q + 2.

These are the squared dipole_geometry amplitudes, normalised to sum to one. ε must be nonzero; its scale does not affect the result.

source
Levels.quadrupole_weights — Method
quadrupole_weights(ε, direction)

Returns the weights $|Γ_q|^2$ with which the beam intensity drives each of the $Δm = q$ electric-quadrupole channels, as a length-5 tuple indexed by q + 3.

Uses quadrupole_geometry with normalised ε and direction. Both must be nonzero and transverse, $ε ⋅ direction = 0$ (tolerance $10^{-3}$ after normalisation). The weights need not sum to one: only the rank-two part of $ε ⊗ direction$ couples.

Levels.resonant_channels — Method

Builds channels for each E2 pair within the structure's manifolds and for the RelativeFrequency reference pair, if present, from the manifold solutions at the structure's field (partners outside the basis are solved into cache). Component positions are relative to the named zero-field interval for the reference pair, or to the centroid interval for other pairs (which cancels in driven mode).

Levels.resonant_state_shift — Method

Sums the signed near-resonant shift of the basis state index over its channels to the other fine-structure manifold, for a laser at δ from the channels' reference and the given geometric weights (w_pol indexed q + 2 for rank 1, w_quad indexed q + 3 for rank 2); exclude drops the channel to one partner state (the resonantly driven component of driven_light_shift). Channels the geometry does not drive are skipped before any division; a driven channel at exactly zero detuning — a degenerate spectator, for which second-order perturbation theory has nothing to expand in — raises an error.

Levels.scalar_polarisability — Method
scalar_polarisability(species, level, frequency)

Returns the zero-field scalar polarisability $α_0(ω)$ at a wavelength or angular frequency; zero frequency gives the static limit. Accepts a fine-structure level of either species kind, or an $F$ level of a hyperfine species. These level curves have no near-resonance guard.

Together with vector_polarisability and tensor_polarisability, this decomposes the polarisability of the sublevel $m$ for polarisation ε as

$α = α_0 + 𝒜 \frac{m}{2j} α_1 + \frac{3 |ε_0|^2 - 1}{2} \frac{3 m^2 - j(j+1)}{j(2j-1)} α_2$,

where ε is normalised, $𝒜 = |ε_{-1}|^2 - |ε_{+1}|^2$ and $ε_0 = ε · ẑ$. The vector term is absent for $j = 0$, the tensor term for $j ≤ 1/2$. polarisability evaluates the state response directly.

For an $F$ level, replaces $j$ by $F$ and extracts the components from the zero-field stretched-state responses: $α_{σ^-} = α_0 - α_1/2 - α_2/2$, $α_π = α_0 + α_2$, $α_{σ^+} = α_0 + α_1/2 - α_2/2$. These single-$F$ factors do not apply to a mixed field eigenstate.

Levels.tensor_expectations — Method

Returns the static remainder's tensor factor in each eigenstate: $⟨(3 J_z^2 - J(J+1)) / (J(2J-1))⟩$, with $J_z$ in units of $ħ$; zero for $J ≤ 1/2$.

The operator is diagonal in the $|m_J, m_I⟩$ product basis. Evaluating it there retains coherences between $F$ components that a weighted average of coupled-state expectations would lose.

Levels.tensor_polarisability — Method
tensor_polarisability(species, level, frequency)

Returns the zero-field tensor polarisability $α_2(ω)$ at a wavelength or angular frequency; see scalar_polarisability for the convention.

Zero when the queried angular momentum ($J$ or $F$) is at most $1/2$. An $F ≥ 1$ level of a $J = 1/2$ parent can have a tensor response from resolved intermediate hyperfine splittings.
