Light shifts and polarisabilities

The light-shift model combines an electric-dipole (E1) background with near-resonant channels resolved into individual Zeeman components. The background uses explicit dipole strengths plus a static scalar/tensor remainder; the channels use Einstein A coefficients and at-field couplings. E1 shifts depend on polarisation. Electric-quadrupole (E2) channels also depend on the beam direction.

State polarisability

polarisability returns the E1 response of a state in SI units ($C m^2/V$). With no polarisation argument, it returns three entries for pure $σ^-$, $π$ and $σ^+$ fields, indexed by $q + 2$. A polarisation argument contracts these with the normalised weights $w_q = |ε_{-q}|^2$. The $π$ entry describes a field along the quantisation axis; the mean of the two $σ$ entries describes a linear field perpendicular to it.

A wavelength or angular frequency selects the dynamic response; 0u"s^-1" selects the static response. For example, the static differential polarisability sets the electric Stark shift from micromotion in a Paul trap:

using Levels, Unitful

s = StateSpec("S_1/2 F=4", 4)
d = StateSpec("D_5/2 F=4", 3)
B0 = insensitive_field(ca43, s => d, (0.45u"mT", 0.55u"mT"))
Δα = polarisability(ca43, d, 0u"s^-1"; B=B0) -
     polarisability(ca43, s, 0u"s^-1"; B=B0)
Δα_parallel = Δα[2]
Δα_perpendicular = (Δα[1] + Δα[3]) / 2
θ = π / 4
Δα_tilted = cos(θ)^2 * Δα_parallel + sin(θ)^2 * Δα_perpendicular
(; Δα_parallel, Δα_perpendicular, Δα_tilted)
(Δα_parallel = -6.558594791951902e-40 C m^2 V^-1, Δα_perpendicular = -7.610019910728565e-40 C m^2 V^-1, Δα_tilted = -7.084307351340233e-40 C m^2 V^-1)

For a sinusoidal electric field with unit polarisation and peak amplitude $E_0$, the cycle-averaged energy shift is $-α E_0^2/4$. For a static field it is $-α E^2/2$. For a light beam of intensity $I$, the angular-frequency shift is $-I α/(2c ε_0 ħ)$.

Without B, states are zero-field $|J,m_J⟩$ or $|F,m_F⟩$ states. With B, hyperfine states denote the adiabatically labelled field eigenstates, including $F$ mixing. Fine-structure eigenvectors remain unchanged, but their Zeeman energies enter the denominators. The scalar/vector/tensor level accessors describe zero-field levels; their single-$F$ angular factors do not apply to mixed field eigenstates.

Parked and driven beams

For a parked beam, light_shift returns the angular-frequency shift of one state. The shift of a transition in a Ramsey measurement is the upper state's shift minus the lower state's shift.

A nearby laser is specified by RelativeFrequency, an angular offset from a named zero-field interval. The uncertain tabulated line centre then cancels from the component detunings, leaving the offset and the Zeeman/hyperfine splittings. See Laser frequencies.

laser = RelativeFrequency(s.level => d.level, 2π * 20u"MHz")
c = LightShiftCoefficients(ca43, [s, d], laser; B=B0)
intensity = 1u"W/m^2"
n, ε = beam_vectors(π / 2, π / 4, 0.3)
Δω_parked = light_shift(c, d, intensity, ε; n) -
            light_shift(c, s, intensity, ε; n)
0.003516387421773259 s^-1

For a resonantly driven component, driven_light_shift returns the displacement of its resonance. It evaluates spectator channels with the laser tuned to the probed component and excludes that component as the coherent drive. A positive result raises the resonance frequency.

Δω_driven = driven_light_shift(c, s => d, intensity, ε; n)
3.97140632784879e-9 μs^-1

Both APIs accept parts = :total | :background | :resonant (default :total). The resonant part needs B and the Einstein A coefficient, but no polarisability data. E2 channels are built for pairs within the basis; E1 channels require that pair to be the RelativeFrequency reference.

Coefficients cache the atomic structure for repeated intensity, polarisation and detuning evaluations. A parked evaluation's δ keyword changes only the near-resonant detunings; the smooth background stays at the construction frequency. Coefficients used in driven mode must have a construction frequency within relative tolerance $10^{-3}$ of the probed line.

Background and channel split

For an E1 reference pair, the near-resonant term moves from the background into the channel sum. The counter-rotating term stays in the background, so the split counts every term once at the construction frequency. The reference channel must be explicit in the LevelPolarisability data; a lumped remainder cannot be split.

Bare frequencies within ~100 GHz of an explicit E1 line are refused: precise component detunings require a reference that cancels the uncertain line centre. The state polarisability accessor has the same guard and rejects RelativeFrequency; the level scalar/vector/tensor accessors remain unguarded curves.

For hyperfine states, explicit E1 channels resolve the known hyperfine splittings of both levels. This can produce a tensor response even for an $F$ level of a $J = 1/2$ parent. With B, amplitudes rotate into both field eigenbases before squaring, and denominators use their eigen-energies. The static remainder is an electronic operator evaluated in the source eigenstate, retaining coherences between its $F$ components.

An intermediate level without hyperfine constants is approximated as degenerate at its centroid, with no Zeeman shift. Field-resolved source levels and near-resonant partners require known constants.

Validity and symmetry

The background requires polarisability data: S$_{1/2}$ and D$_{5/2}$ for sr88, and also D$_{3/2}$ for ca40 and ca43. Evaluating a background for a level without data raises an error; its near-resonant channels can still be used with parts = :resonant. The static remainder approximates unresolved E1 channels and is suitable only far from their resonances. E2 contributions outside the selected near-resonant pair and counter-rotating E2 terms are omitted.

The channel sum uses second-order perturbation theory with unregularised $1/detuning$ poles. Evaluation warns if a channel's Rabi frequency exceeds one tenth of its detuning or the laser lies within five upper-level linewidths of a pole. Linewidths enter these warnings, not the denominators; the model provides no scattering rate.

For fine-structure E2 transitions, spectator detunings scale with B, so the resonant shift scales as $I/B$. With linear polarisation, reversing both magnetic projections changes this part's sign; it cancels in a $±m$ pair average ([Lindvall2025], Sec. III F 2). Ellipticity breaks that cancellation. The E1 background generally remains in the pair average.

Hyperfine spectators in other $F$ levels generally prevent the resonant pair cancellation and the $I/B$ scaling. For linear polarisation, the field-reversal identity is $shift(-m,B) = shift(+m,-B)$, with both projections reversed; for elliptical polarisation the field reversal also requires conjugating ε.

Levels.BACKGROUND_GUARD_WINDOW — Constant

A bare laser frequency closer than this to an explicit electric-dipole channel requires a RelativeFrequency reference: its channel detunings must cancel the uncertain tabulated line centre.

Levels.LightShiftCoefficients — Method
LightShiftCoefficients(species, basis::StateBasis, laser[; B])
LightShiftCoefficients(species, levels_or_states::AbstractVector, laser[; B])

Precomputes the ac Stark shift data of every state in the given basis, for a laser given as a wavelength, an angular frequency, or a RelativeFrequency.

Caches the atomic structure for repeated intensity, polarisation and detuning evaluations, for example in laser-parameter fits.

The E1 background requires LevelPolarisability data. Levels without it are admitted, but evaluating their background raises. With B, builds near-resonant channels for each E2 pair within the basis and the RelativeFrequency reference pair (E1 or E2). Channels need only Einstein A coefficients. Evaluates with light_shift for a parked beam, or driven_light_shift for a resonantly driven component.

A RelativeFrequency laser requires B. For an E1 reference pair, moves the near-resonant term from the background into the channels; that pair must be explicit in the polarisability data. Bare frequencies within ~100 GHz of an explicit E1 line are refused because precise detunings require cancellation of the uncertain tabulated line centre.

With B, the background describes field eigenstates, including hyperfine $F$ mixing and Zeeman energies; without it, zero-field states. See polarisability for the conventions and intermediate-state fallback.

Levels.ResonantChannel — Type

One near-resonant coupling channel of a basis state: a Zeeman component connecting it to partner in the other manifold of a level pair the laser sits close to.

The signed weight is such that the second-order shift of the state from this channel is weight × intensity × w / (δ − Δ), with w the geometric weight of its $Δm = q$ component (polarisation_weights[q + 2] for rank 1, quadrupole_weights[q + 3] for rank 2) and δ the laser offset from the same reference Δ is measured against. It is positive for a state in the lower manifold of the pair and negative for one in the upper (level repulsion away from the driving photon); 4 × |weight| × intensity × w is the squared Rabi frequency of the component, and γ the total decay rate of the upper level — both used by the validity warnings at evaluation time.

Levels.channel_scalar_polarisability — Method

Returns one E1 channel's scalar polarisability at photon energy ħω: $2Δ d^2 / [3(2j+1)(Δ^2-ħω^2)]$, for source angular momentum j, reduced dipole d and signed intermediate-level energy difference Δ.

Also supplies the static contribution subtracted when resolving an ImplicitPolarisability, keeping the anchor and evaluation consistent.

Levels.driven_light_shift — Method
driven_light_shift(coefficients::LightShiftCoefficients, transition::Pair, intensity, ε; n, parts)
driven_light_shift(species, lower::StateSpec, upper::StateSpec, intensity, ε; n, B, parts)

Returns the angular-frequency displacement of the lower => upper resonance under a laser tuned to that component. A positive result raises the resonance frequency.

The background part is the difference of the two states' far-detuned electric-dipole shifts. The resonant part sums, for both states, the couplings to every other Zeeman component sharing one of them — the driven channel itself is the coherent drive, not a shift — with the laser pinned to the probed component, so the laser frequency drops out and only exact at-field splittings enter the detunings. parts selects :total (the default), :background or :resonant.

For an E2 line, parts = :resonant is the "E2 ac Stark shift" of [Lindvall2025], Sec. III F 2. In a fine-structure species, linear polarisation makes this part odd under reversal of both projections, so it cancels in a $±m$ pair average. Ellipticity breaks this cancellation. For hyperfine states, spectators in other $F$ levels generally prevent it; with linear polarisation the field-reversal identity is instead $shift(-m, B) = shift(+m, -B)$, with both projections reversed.

The probed pair may be any electric-quadrupole transition with channels in the coefficients (which requires construction with B); an electric-dipole pair must additionally be the RelativeFrequency reference pair. The beam direction n is required for electric-quadrupole pairs and unused for electric-dipole ones. For the background part, the coefficients' laser frequency must be consistent with the probed transition (rtol $10^{-3}$) — in driven mode the laser is on that line. Like the parked light_shift, the resonant sum is second-order perturbation theory and warns when a spectator Rabi frequency approaches its detuning (which breaks down first for near-degenerate components at very low fields).

Levels.light_shift — Method
light_shift(coefficients::LightShiftCoefficients, state::StateSpec, intensity, ε; n, δ, parts)
light_shift(species, state::StateSpec, laser, intensity, ε; n, B, δ, parts)

Returns the ac Stark (light) shift of the given state (in angular units) for a beam of the given intensity and polarisation ε, parked at the laser frequency the coefficients were built for.

The shift is the far-detuned electric-dipole background — which depends on the polarisation alone, never on the beam direction — plus, for a RelativeFrequency laser (necessarily built with B), the near-resonant sum over the Zeeman components connecting the state to the other manifold of the reference pair, at their exact at-field detunings from the stated laser offset. For an electric-quadrupole reference pair the beam direction n is required, as the quadrupole coupling depends on it; for an electric-dipole one it is not used. parts selects :total (the default), :background or :resonant.

δ overrides the RelativeFrequency offset in the channel sum. The smooth background remains at the construction frequency; rebuild the coefficients if its variation matters over a sweep.

The change a parked beam makes to the splitting of a lower => upper pair — a Ramsey-type measurement, with no component resonantly driven — is the difference of the two states' shifts; the displacement of the resonance observed when driving a component is driven_light_shift instead.

The near-resonant sum is second-order perturbation theory: every channel Rabi frequency must stay small against its detuning, and detunings within a few linewidths of a pole are outside the model; both conditions warn when violated. The one-shot species form is a convenience wrapper that rebuilds the coefficients on every call.

B also selects field eigenstates for the background, including for a bare frequency or wavelength. Without B, it describes zero-field states; see polarisability.

Levels.manifold_polarisabilities — Method

Returns a length(source.basis) × 3 matrix of E1 polarisabilities, indexed by [state, q + 2] like polarisation_weights. Each intermediate state contributes $|d_{ts}|^2 [w_q/(E_t-E_s-ħω) + w_{-q}/(E_t-E_s+ħω)]$, with dipole matrix element $d_{ts}$ and energies at the common field B. Adds the static scalar remainder and the tensor remainder weighted by Levels.tensor_expectations.

exclude names an explicit intermediate level whose near-resonant term moves into LightShiftCoefficients's channel sum. The counter-rotating term stays here; a lumped remainder cannot be split.

Levels.manifold_solution — Method

Returns the solution for fine-structure level fs at field B (nothing for zero field), cached once per coefficient construction.

Fine-structure states retain their basis vectors and acquire Zeeman energies. Hyperfine states use hyperfine_manifold with B, or coupled states with Casimir energies at zero field. An intermediate without hyperfine constants stays degenerate at its centroid, with no Zeeman shift; callers use Levels.validate_field_solution where this fallback is forbidden.

Levels.polarisability — Method
polarisability(species, state::StateSpec, frequency[, ε]; B=nothing)

Returns one state's E1 polarisability in SI units ($C m^2/V$). Without ε, returns a length-3 vector for pure $σ^-$, $π$ and $σ^+$ driving fields, indexed by q + 2. With ε, contracts that vector with polarisation_weights; the scale of ε is ignored. A real ε specifies a linearly oscillating field: the $π$ entry applies along ẑ, and the mean of the $σ$ entries applies perpendicular to it.

frequency is the wavelength or angular frequency of the driving field — a laser or a trap rf drive; 0u"s^-1" gives the static response. Frequencies within ~100 GHz of an explicit E1 line are refused because precise detunings require cancellation of the uncertain line centre. That regime is handled by LightShiftCoefficients and light_shift; this accessor rejects RelativeFrequency.

Without B, the state is a zero-field $|J, m_J⟩$ or $|F, m_F⟩$ state. With B (signed, finite, nonzero scalar flux density along ẑ), a hyperfine state denotes the adiabatically labelled eigenstate, including $F$ mixing. Fine-structure eigenvectors are unchanged. The sum resolves known hyperfine splittings and, with B, Zeeman energies in both manifolds. An intermediate without hyperfine constants stays degenerate at its centroid, with no Zeeman shift. The static remainder is evaluated as an electronic operator in the source state.

Returns the diagonal second-order E1 response, including rotating and counter-rotating terms and a static remainder; excludes E2 channels. For a sinusoidal field of peak amplitude E, the cycle-averaged energy shift is $-α E^2/4$; for a static field it is $-α E^2/2$. For light intensity I, the angular shift is $-I α/(2c ε_0 ħ)$.

Levels.polarisation_weights — Method
polarisation_weights(ε)

Returns the fractions $w_q = |ε_{-q}|^2$ of the beam intensity driving each of the $Δm = q$ electric-dipole channels, as a length-3 tuple indexed by q + 2.

These are the squared dipole_geometry amplitudes, normalised to sum to one. ε must be nonzero; its scale does not affect the result.

Levels.quadrupole_weights — Method
quadrupole_weights(ε, n)

Returns the weights $|Γ_q|^2$ with which the beam intensity drives each of the $Δm = q$ electric-quadrupole channels, as a length-5 tuple indexed by q + 3.

Uses quadrupole_geometry with normalised ε and n. Both must be nonzero and transverse, $ε ⋅ n = 0$ (tolerance $10^{-3}$ after normalisation). The weights need not sum to one: only the rank-two part of $ε ⊗ n$ couples.

Levels.scalar_polarisability — Method
scalar_polarisability(species, level, laser)

Returns the zero-field scalar polarisability $α_0(ω)$ at a wavelength or angular frequency; zero frequency gives the static limit. Accepts a fine-structure level of either species kind, or an $F$ level of a hyperfine species. These level curves have no near-resonance guard.

Together with vector_polarisability and tensor_polarisability, this decomposes the polarisability of the sublevel $m$ for polarisation ε as

$α = α_0 + 𝒜 \frac{m}{2j} α_1 + \frac{3 |ε_0|^2 - 1}{2} \frac{3 m^2 - j(j+1)}{j(2j-1)} α_2$,

where ε is normalised, $𝒜 = |ε_{-1}|^2 - |ε_{+1}|^2$ and $ε_0 = ε · ẑ$. The vector term is absent for $j = 0$, the tensor term for $j ≤ 1/2$. polarisability evaluates the state response directly.

For an $F$ level, replaces $j$ by $F$ and extracts the components from the zero-field stretched-state responses: $α_{σ^-} = α_0 - α_1/2 - α_2/2$, $α_π = α_0 + α_2$, $α_{σ^+} = α_0 + α_1/2 - α_2/2$. These single-$F$ factors do not apply to a mixed field eigenstate.

Levels.tensor_expectations — Method

Returns the static remainder's tensor factor in each eigenstate: $⟨(3 J_z^2 - J(J+1)) / (J(2J-1))⟩$, with $J_z$ in units of $ħ$; zero for $J ≤ 1/2$.

The operator is diagonal in the $|m_J, m_I⟩$ product basis. Evaluating it there retains coherences between $F$ components that a weighted average of coupled-state expectations would lose.
