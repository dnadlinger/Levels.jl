Species and transitions

An atomic species is described by its level energies and the Einstein A coefficients connecting them; transition frequencies, level lifetimes and saturation intensities are derived from those two tables. A species with nuclear spin additionally carries its hyperfine coupling constants, nuclear g-factor and measured electronic g-factors, while the energy and rate tables stay keyed on the fine-structure levels — hyperfine level arguments resolve to those, with the zero-field hyperfine shifts added where the quantity is frequency-like (cf. the hyperfine-structure reference).

Transition frequencies are angular frequencies; Einstein A coefficients are decay rates, with no factor of 2π. See Frequencies, rates and energies.

Levels.BOHR_RADIUS — Constant

Bohr radius $a_0 = 4 π ε_0 ħ^2 / (m_e e^2)$.

Levels.DIPOLE_AU — Constant

Atomic unit of the electric dipole moment, $e a_0$.

Reduced matrix elements are conventionally quoted in these units in the literature; see LevelPolarisability.

source
Levels.POLARISABILITY_AU — Constant

Atomic unit of the electric polarisability, $e^2 a_0^2 / E_h$.

Polarisabilities are conventionally quoted in these units in the literature; see LevelPolarisability.

source
Levels.ElectricDipole — Type

The strength of an electric-dipole (E1) transition, specified either as its Einstein A coefficient or through the absolute reduced dipole matrix element $|⟨\mathrm{upper}‖d‖\mathrm{lower}⟩|$.

Accepted as a value in the einstein_as table by the species keyword constructors. A matrix element is converted to the equivalent rate (Levels.einstein_a_from_electric_dipole, with the frequency from the level energies): the form for pairs whose best-determined literature quantity is the matrix element (typically from a many-body calculation) rather than a directly measured rate. A rate is equivalent to a plain einstein_as entry, which parity already marks as E1, but states the multipole explicitly. Downstream only the rate exists; there is one strength per transition, however it was specified.

source
Levels.ElectricDipole — Method
ElectricDipole(strength)

Creates the E1 strength specification from an Einstein A coefficient (an inverse time, e.g. 1.4e8u"s^-1") or a reduced matrix element in any dipole-moment units (e.g. 4.187 * Levels.DIPOLE_AU).

source
Levels.HyperfineConstants — Type

Hyperfine coupling constants of one fine-structure level, stored as energies (i.e. $h$ times the conventionally quoted frequency values).

The magnetic-dipole constant a and electric-quadrupole constant b enter the level Hamiltonian as $A \, \vec{I} ⋅ \vec{J}$ plus the standard Casimir quadrupole term (cf. hyperfine_shift); b must be zero unless both $I > 1/2$ and $J > 1/2$.

source
Levels.HyperfineConstants — Method
HyperfineConstants(; a, b = zero(a))

Creates the hyperfine coupling constants of one level, promoting the two energies to a common type.

source
Levels.HyperfineOneElectronSpecies — Type

Atomic species with only one (relevant) electron – all configurations spin-1/2 – and hyperfine structure from a nuclear spin $I$.

The level data (energies, einstein_as, polarisabilities) is keyed on the fine-structure levels exactly as for NoHyperfineOneElectronSpecies — energies are hyperfine centroids, and Einstein A coefficients are fine-structure rates (each hyperfine sublevel decays at the full fine-structure rate, so there are never $F$-resolved entries). The hyperfine structure itself is described by nuclear_spin, nuclear_g and the per-level hyperfine coupling constants.

source
Levels.HyperfineOneElectronSpecies — Method
HyperfineOneElectronSpecies(; mass, nuclear_spin, nuclear_g, energies, hyperfine,
    einstein_as, lande_g_overrides = Dict(), polarisabilities = Dict())

Creates the species from its level data.

As for NoHyperfineOneElectronSpecies, einstein_as entries may be plain rates or ElectricDipole and MagneticDipole strengths, and polarisabilities may mix LevelPolarisability values with ImplicitPolarisability specifications; both are resolved here.

source
Levels.ImplicitPolarisability — Type

E1 channels and total static polarisabilities for one fine-structure level. Species constructors resolve these into LevelPolarisability data, subtracting the explicit channels' static contributions from each total.

A bare channel level derives its dipole from the species' Einstein A coefficient. An explicit level => dipole is accepted only if the pair is absent from einstein_as, so each transition has one independent strength. Rate tables should remain complete for each upper level because they also determine lifetime.

The remainder includes any mismatch between the chosen dipoles and the data underlying the static anchors; it need not represent a physical tail alone.

source
Levels.ImplicitPolarisability — Method
ImplicitPolarisability(channels; static_scalar, static_tensor = nothing)

Creates the light-shift specification for one level from a collection of intermediate levels — each a bare level (in spectroscopic notation or as a NoHyperfineNumberSpec; dipole derived from the Einstein A coefficient) or a level => dipole pair — and the total static polarisabilities.

Quantities accept any compatible units, e.g. 74.62 * Levels.POLARISABILITY_AU. An anchor of nothing leaves the corresponding remainder zero; an anchor of zero makes the total zero. static_scalar is required, while static_tensor defaults to nothing. For $J ≤ 1/2$, the electronic tensor remainder vanishes; resolved hyperfine channels can still give an $F$ level a tensor response.

source
Levels.LevelPolarisability — Type

Electric-dipole data for the ac Stark shift (light shift) of one level.

Explicit intermediate levels contribute their frequency-dependent E1 sums; the ionic core and omitted channels contribute a static scalar/tensor remainder. This approximation requires the driving field to be far from the omitted resonances. Used by polarisability and light_shift.

source
Levels.LevelPolarisability — Method
LevelPolarisability(reduced_dipoles; static_scalar, static_tensor)

Creates light-shift data from level => reduced matrix element pairs and static remainders, not total polarisabilities. Both remainders default to zero; ImplicitPolarisability accepts total static anchors instead.

Levels may be given in spectroscopic notation, and all quantities are converted to the stored units, so literature values can be written as e.g. 3.078 * Levels.DIPOLE_AU.

source
Levels.MagneticDipole — Type

The strength of a magnetic-dipole (M1) transition, specified either as its Einstein A coefficient or through the absolute reduced matrix element $|⟨\mathrm{upper}‖μ‖\mathrm{lower}⟩|$ of the magnetic moment.

Accepted as a value in the einstein_as table by the species keyword constructors, which record the pair as M1 (cf. Levels.multipole): a same-parity pair is otherwise taken as an electric-quadrupole transition. The typical use is the decay between fine-structure partners, such as D$_{5/2}$ → D$_{3/2}$, for which a plain rate is refused as ambiguous. A matrix element is converted to the equivalent rate (Levels.einstein_a_from_magnetic_dipole, with the frequency from the level energies). Downstream only that rate exists, and it fixes the decay (lifetimes, jump operators) and the coherent coupling (rabi_frequency) of the pair alike.

source

MagneticDipole(strength)

Creates the M1 strength specification from an Einstein A coefficient (an inverse time, e.g. 5.6e-3u"s^-1") or a reduced matrix element in magnetic-moment units (e.g. 1.55u"μB").

Levels.NoHyperfineOneElectronSpecies — Method
NoHyperfineOneElectronSpecies(; mass, energies, einstein_as,
    lande_g_overrides = Dict(), polarisabilities = Dict())

Creates the species from its level data.

einstein_as entries may be plain Einstein A coefficients, taken as the lowest electric multipole parity allows, or ElectricDipole and MagneticDipole strengths, each a rate or a reduced matrix element; a magnetic-dipole transition must be given as a MagneticDipole. polarisabilities may mix LevelPolarisability values with ImplicitPolarisability specifications; both are resolved against the level energies (and, for the latter, the resolved A coefficients) here.

Levels.OneElectronSpecies — Type

Atomic species with only one (relevant) electron, i.e. all configurations spin-1/2.

Concrete subtypes are NoHyperfineOneElectronSpecies and HyperfineOneElectronSpecies; the level-data accessors (Levels.transition_frequency, einstein_a, lifetime, saturation_intensity, level_polarisability) are shared between them, with fine-structure data keyed on the NoHyperfineNumberSpec part of the queried level.

Levels.einstein_a — Method
einstein_a(species::OneElectronSpecies, lower => upper)

Returns the Einstein A coefficient for the given two levels.

The coefficient is a decay rate in inverse time, without a factor of 2π. Summing over all lower levels gives the inverse upper-level lifetime, equal in value to its natural angular linewidth. Hyperfine levels are resolved to their fine-structure part, as A coefficients are fine-structure quantities (the $F$-resolved rates are branching fractions of them; cf. Levels.hyperfine_reduction).

Returns nothing if there is no decay from upper to lower.

source
Levels.einstein_a_from_electric_dipole — Method

Returns the Einstein A coefficient of the electric-dipole (E1) transition with (angular) frequency ω, upper-level angular momentum j_upper and absolute reduced dipole matrix element d, $A = ω^3 d^2 / (3 π ε_0 ħ c^3 (2 j_\mathrm{upper} + 1))$.

source
Levels.einstein_a_from_magnetic_dipole — Method

Returns the Einstein A coefficient of the magnetic-dipole (M1) transition with (angular) frequency ω, upper-level angular momentum j_upper and absolute reduced magnetic-moment matrix element μ, $A = ω^3 μ^2 / (3 π ε_0 ħ c^5 (2 j_\mathrm{upper} + 1))$ — the electric-dipole expression of Levels.einstein_a_from_electric_dipole with $d → μ/c$.

source
Levels.electric_dipole_from_einstein_a — Method

Returns the absolute reduced dipole matrix element implied by the Einstein A coefficient a (the inverse of Levels.einstein_a_from_electric_dipole).

Levels.lifetime — Method
lifetime(species::OneElectronSpecies, level)

Returns the lifetime of the given level.

This is the reciprocal of the sum of all the decay rates from the level. Hyperfine levels are resolved to their fine-structure part — every hyperfine sublevel decays at the full fine-structure rate.

Returns nothing if there are no decay channels defined from the given level.

Levels.saturation_intensity — Method
saturation_intensity(species::OneElectronSpecies, lower => upper)

Returns the saturation intensity in the Oxford ion-trap group's convention ([SATSUM]):

\[I_0 = \frac{ħ ω^3}{6π c^2 τ} = \frac{4π^2 ħ c}{3λ^3 τ},\]

where $ω$ is the pair's zero-field angular frequency and $τ$ the total upper-level lifetime. A tabulated Einstein A coefficient for the pair is not required; unknown levels, reversed energy order or missing upper-level decay data raise an error.

For a fine-structure E1 pair at unit geometric amplitude, $|Ω|^2 = C^2 (I/I_0) A/τ$, with $C$ the Clebsch–Gordan coefficient. On a closed stretched transition, $I = I_0$ gives $|Ω| = Γ = 1/τ$. Thus $I_0 = 2 I_\mathrm{sat}$ for the two-level convention $s = I/I_\mathrm{sat} = 2|Ω|^2/Γ^2$ of [Foot2005].

References

  • [SATSUM]: A. M. Steane, "A summary of saturation intensities, or: How to find scattering rates and Rabi frequencies quickly", internal Oxford ion-trap group note (2001-12-03).
  • [Foot2005]: C. J. Foot, "Atomic Physics", Oxford University Press (2005), §7.6.1; also Metcalf & van der Straten, "Laser Cooling and Trapping" (1999), §2.4.

Levels.transition_frequency — Method
transition_frequency(species::OneElectronSpecies, lower => upper)

Returns the zero-field angular frequency of the given transition.

For hyperfine levels (of a HyperfineOneElectronSpecies), the zero-field hyperfine shifts of any $F$-resolved levels are included on top of the centroid splitting. A pair of states gives the zero-field interval of their levels (their Zeeman components are degenerate there); the interval at a static field is transition_frequency(s::Eigenstructure, lower => upper).

Both levels must have tabulated energies; a decay channel between them is not required. A pair of levels must be in increasing energy order. A pair of states returns the signed upper-minus-lower interval, including within one fine-structure manifold.
