Species and transitions

An atomic species is described by its level energies and the Einstein A coefficients connecting them; transition frequencies, level lifetimes and saturation intensities are derived from those two tables. A species with nuclear spin additionally carries its hyperfine coupling constants, nuclear g-factor and measured electronic g-factors, while the energy and rate tables stay keyed on the fine-structure levels — hyperfine level arguments resolve to those, with the zero-field hyperfine shifts added where the quantity is frequency-like (cf. the hyperfine-structure reference).

Frequencies and rates are in angular units throughout.

Levels.BOHR_RADIUS — Constant

Bohr radius $a_0 = 4 π ε_0 ħ^2 / (m_e e^2)$.

source
Levels.DIPOLE_AU — Constant

Atomic unit of the electric dipole moment, $e a_0$.

Reduced matrix elements are conventionally quoted in these units in the literature; see LevelPolarisability.

source
Levels.POLARIZABILITY_AU — Constant

Atomic unit of the electric polarisability, $e^2 a_0^2 / E_h$.

Polarisabilities are conventionally quoted in these units in the literature; see LevelPolarisability.

source
Levels.HyperfineConstants — Type

Hyperfine coupling constants of one fine-structure level, stored as energies (i.e. $h$ times the conventionally quoted frequency values).

The magnetic-dipole constant a and electric-quadrupole constant b enter the level Hamiltonian as $A \, \vec{I} ⋅ \vec{J}$ plus the standard Casimir quadrupole term (cf. hyperfine_shift); b must be zero unless both $I > 1/2$ and $J > 1/2$.

source
Levels.HyperfineConstants — Method
HyperfineConstants(; a, b = zero(a))

Creates the hyperfine coupling constants of one level, promoting the two energies to a common type.

source
Levels.HyperfineOneElectronSpecies — Type

Atomic species with only one (relevant) electron – all configurations spin-1/2 – and hyperfine structure from a nuclear spin $I$.

The level data (energies, einstein_as, polarisabilities) is keyed on the fine-structure levels exactly as for NoHyperfineOneElectronSpecies — energies are hyperfine centroids, and Einstein A coefficients are fine-structure rates (each hyperfine sublevel decays at the full fine-structure rate, so there are never $F$-resolved entries). The hyperfine structure itself is described by nuclear_spin, nuclear_g and the per-level hyperfine coupling constants.

source
Levels.LevelPolarisability — Type

Electric-dipole data for the ac Stark shift (light shift) of one level.

The shift is evaluated as a sum over the intermediate levels an electric-dipole transition connects to (cf. light_shift). The few channels that dominate are given explicitly by their reduced matrix elements, so that their detuning from the driving laser is accounted for; everything else — the ionic core, and the many weak transitions to high-lying levels — is lumped into a pair of static polarisabilities, which is a good approximation as long as the laser is far detuned from those transitions.

source
Levels.LevelPolarisability — Method
LevelPolarisability(reduced_dipoles; static_scalar, static_tensor)

Creates the light-shift data for one level from a collection of level => reduced matrix element pairs and the static remainder.

Levels may be given in spectroscopic notation, and all quantities are converted to the stored units, so literature values can be written as e.g. 3.078 * Levels.DIPOLE_AU.

source
Levels.NoHyperfineOneElectronSpecies — Type

Atomic species with only one (relevant) electron – all configurations spin-1/2 – and no hyperfine structure.

source
Levels.OneElectronSpecies — Type

Atomic species with only one (relevant) electron, i.e. all configurations spin-1/2.

Concrete subtypes are NoHyperfineOneElectronSpecies and HyperfineOneElectronSpecies; the level-data accessors (Levels.transition_frequency, einstein_a, lifetime, saturation_intensity, level_polarisability) are shared between them, with fine-structure data keyed on the NoHyperfineNumberSpec part of the queried level.

Levels.einstein_a — Method
einstein_a(species::OneElectronSpecies, lower, upper)

Returns the Einstein A coefficient for the given two levels.

The sum of all A coefficients for a given upper level is the reciprocal level lifetime, so this is the linewidth contribution in angular units. Hyperfine levels are resolved to their fine-structure part, as A coefficients are fine-structure quantities (the $F$-resolved rates are branching fractions of them; cf. Levels.hyperfine_reduction).

Returns nothing if there is no decay from upper to lower.

source
Levels.level_energy — Method

Returns the energy of the given level relative to the species' reference point: the stored (centroid) energy for a fine-structure level, plus the zero-field hyperfine shift for a hyperfine level.

source
Levels.level_polarisability — Method
level_polarisability(species::OneElectronSpecies, level)

Returns the LevelPolarisability light-shift data for the given level (the fine-structure part for hyperfine levels).

Returns nothing if no such data is known for the level.

source
Levels.lifetime — Method
lifetime(species::OneElectronSpecies, level)

Returns the lifetime of the given level.

This is the reciprocal of the sum of all the decay rates from the level. Hyperfine levels are resolved to their fine-structure part — every hyperfine sublevel decays at the full fine-structure rate.

Returns nothing if there are no decay channels defined from the given level.

source
Levels.saturation_intensity — Method
saturation_intensity(species::OneElectronSpecies, lower, upper)

Returns the saturation intensity for the transition between the two levels.

An error is raised if the two levels are not connected by a known transition.

source
Levels.transition_frequency — Method
transition_frequency(species::OneElectronSpecies, lower, upper)

Returns the frequency of a given transition (in angular units).

For hyperfine levels (of a HyperfineOneElectronSpecies), the zero-field hyperfine shifts of any $F$-resolved levels are included on top of the centroid splitting.

An error is raised if the two levels are not connected by a known transition or if the levels are incorrectly ordered.

source
Levels.validate_hyperfine — Method

Checks that the $F$ quantum number of the given hyperfine level is compatible with the nuclear spin of the species, returning the level.
