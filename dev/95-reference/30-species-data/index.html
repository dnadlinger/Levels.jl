Atomic data

Level structure data for the supported species — ⁸⁸Sr⁺ (sr88), ⁴⁰Ca⁺ (ca40), ⁴³Ca⁺ (ca43) and ¹³⁸Ba⁺ (ba138) — with literature citations for every value. The Ca⁺ isotopes share rate and g-factor measurements from ⁴⁰Ca⁺ and a common polarisability model; masses, level energies and hyperfine data are specified per isotope.

Levels.CA_EINSTEIN_AS — Constant

Einstein A coefficients of the Ca⁺ ion; all lifetimes and branching fractions were measured in ⁴⁰Ca⁺, the isotope dependence being far below the quoted uncertainties.

source
Levels.CA_LANDE_G_OVERRIDES — Constant

Measured electronic g-factors of the Ca⁺ ion (⁴⁰Ca⁺ measurements).

source
Levels.CA_POLARISABILITIES — Constant

Electric-dipole light-shift data of the Ca⁺ ion (cf. ImplicitPolarisability; resolved per isotope against its level energies and the shared Einstein A coefficients).

source
Levels.ba138 — Constant

¹³⁸Ba⁺ ion.

The P decay rates are entered as the reduced dipole matrix elements of [JLAB26b], an unrefereed preprint, whose ⟨P₁/₂‖r‖S₁/₂⟩ lies 1.8σ below the value Jayjong et al. [JLAB26a] infers from the static S₁/₂ polarisability. They are preferred over τ(P₃/₂) = 6.2615(72) ns of [ZAC+20], which rests on the older ⟨P₃/₂‖r‖S₁/₂⟩ of [WHLS10], whose ratio to the P₁/₂ element disagrees with the direct measurement of [JLAB26a].

D₅/₂ decays to S₁/₂ by E2 and, with a 17 % branch, to D₃/₂ by M1 (a MagneticDipole entry). Its lifetime is thus the measured 30.14(40) s [ZAC+20], and a model holding D₅/₂ must also hold D₃/₂, or drop that channel explicitly (open_channels = :drop).

The 4f levels are not included, as the sources give neither their energies nor dipoles. Their contribution to α(D₅/₂) is lumped into the static remainder, which is a good approximation only well below their ≈ 234 nm resonances: with the ≈ 15 a.u. ultraviolet term of [JLAB26a, Eq. (1)], the error is ≈ 0.2 a.u. at 1762 nm, ≈ 2 a.u. at 653 nm and ≈ 4 a.u. at 481 nm, which limits the accuracy of the differential polarisability near its zero crossings.

Further sources, cited per value in the comments of src/species_data.jl: [AKC+20, WSMS94, IKK+19, CR25, MTW98, KMH+96, PR76, SL07, WHK+21, KRR{24].

source
Levels.ca40 — Constant

⁴⁰Ca⁺ ion (no nuclear spin).

The level energies are the directly measured ⁴⁰Ca⁺ transition frequencies from S$_{1/2}$: the 729 nm clock transition [ZHZ+23], from which D$_{3/2}$ follows via the frequency-comb Raman measurement of the 3d fine-structure interval [SMF+18], and the 397 nm [GWW+15] and 393 nm [SGG+17] lines. As a consistency check, the implied 866, 850 and 854 nm intervals agree with the collinear-spectroscopy values of [MKI+20, Table III] to within 0.2 MHz (1.5σ). The Einstein A coefficients and electronic g-factors measured in ⁴⁰Ca⁺, and the polarisability model combining measured dipoles with calculated static anchors, are shared with ca43.

Further sources, cited per value in the comments of src/species_data.jl: [WHK+21, PSB22, TPR+03, CBK+09, MZH+24, ZMH+26, MSG+26, SHG+16, SHG+17, SHGG18, HRK+15, RPK+13, GKZ+08, MSSW20, KBL+05, YS25, TQSM13].

source
Levels.ca43 — Constant

⁴³Ca⁺ ion (nuclear spin $I = 7/2$).

Hyperfine centroids are referenced to the S$_{1/2}$ centroid; all hyperfine $A$/$B$ constants, including their signs, follow the cited measurements. The electronic g-factors and Einstein A coefficients marked as such are ⁴⁰Ca⁺ measurements, entered uncorrected. For the Einstein A coefficients and g(S$_{1/2}$) the isotope dependence is far below the quoted uncertainties; for g(D$_{5/2}$), known to 5 × 10⁻⁸ in ⁴⁰Ca⁺, the leading-order reduced-mass shift (cf. Levels.ls_lande_g) is not, but is deliberately not applied: the many-electron recoil corrections are unknown at that level, so will have to be measured together with the hyperfine constants anyway.

The level energies are the hyperfine-centroid transition frequencies of [Kra20]. The hyperfine constants are from [ABG+94, NBI+98] and, for D$_{5/2}$, [BKR+07a], with the signs per the erratum [BKR+07b]; the nuclear g-factor follows from the moment of [HAH+21]. Further sources, cited per value in the comments of src/species_data.jl: [WHK+21, PSB22, TPR+03, CBK+09, MZH+24, ZMH+26, MSG+26, SHG+16, SHG+17, SHGG18, HRK+15, RPK+13, GKZ+08, MSSW20, KBL+05, YS25, TQSM13].

source
Levels.sr88 — Constant

⁸⁸Sr⁺ ion.

The data are taken from [San12, LTGG16, WHK+21, SN10, JASC09, MPG+26, Bar12, HNA+13, MTW98]; the comments in src/species_data.jl give the source of each value.

source
Levels.ca_ion_mass — Method

Mass of a Ca⁺ ion from the neutral-atom mass: less one electron, plus the Ca I first ionisation energy 49305.919611(4) cm⁻¹ [PSB22] as its mass-equivalent binding correction (NB: as this is well below the [WHK+21] mass uncertainties, the usefulness of this is questionable already, so the isotope shifts on the ionisation energy are especially negligible here).
