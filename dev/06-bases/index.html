Bases and representations

A matrix's basis determines what its entries mean. This page describes the index spaces and phase conventions used by Levels, and how to construct operators in them.

Three bases

BasisLabelsWhere it appears
Product ($m_J, m_I$ kets)electronic and nuclear projections; storage order electronic slow, nuclear fast, each increasingconstruction device only: Levels.moment_operators (the magnetic moment is a Kronecker sum there) and the tensor remainder of the polarisability
Coupled ($F, m_F$ kets; $J, m_J$ for a fine-structure species)zero-field quantum numbers; electron-first coupling $⟨J m_J; I m_I ∣ F m_F⟩$, Condon–Shortley phasescoupled_basis(s); every zero-field species-form result; the natural basis for an operator written down from quantum numbers
Field eigenbasisadiabatic $(F, m_F)$ labels at field(s) where the zero-field branches are resolved ($m_F$ exact)basis(s); operator factories and model inputs taking a structure

For a fine-structure species the three coincide ($U = I$; $W = I$ for a canonical complete basis), so its angular amplitudes need no rotation in the fixed-$J$ model; its energies and energy-dependent responses still depend on the field. Partial or reordered bases still select or permute, even at zero.

Two matrices

  • $U$ = Levels.coupling_transform(species, level): $U_{(m_J, m_I),(F, m_F)} = ⟨J m_J; I m_I | F m_F⟩$, real, nonzero only where $m_J + m_I = m_F$, for one manifold. Product → coupled: $X_c = U^† X_p U$.
  • $W$ = eigenvectors(s): column $k$ holds the coupled-basis components of the eigenstate labelled basis(s)[k]; rows are indexed by coupled_basis(s). Coupled → eigen: $X_e = W^† X_c W$, which is to_eigenbasis(s, X_c).

Within one manifold, an operator in the product basis transforms as $X_e = W^† U^† X_p U W$, with $W$ restricted to that manifold's rows and selected columns. For a structure spanning several manifolds, embed the coupled operator into coupled_basis(s) before calling to_eigenbasis.

using Levels, LinearAlgebra, Unitful
s = Eigenstructure(ca43, "S_1/2", "D_5/2"; B=0.5u"mT")
W = eigenvectors(s)
size(W), maximum(abs, W' * W - I)
((64, 64), 9.373498386299306e-16)

The representation contract

Operator factories and model constructors taking a structure use its eigenbasis, indexed by basis(s) (row and column $i$ ↔ basis(s)[i]). Species-form operator factories use the coupled basis in the supplied StateBasis order. Conversion and diagnostic functions explicitly name both of their index spaces: to_eigenbasis(s, X) accepts X over coupled_basis(s); eigenvectors(s) has coupled rows and selected eigenstate columns; coupled_hamiltonian(s) returns a coupled-basis operator.

Model constructors take their input matrices in that convention and perform no automatic rotation. A plain matrix carries no basis metadata: equal sizes cannot distinguish matrices from different bases, fields, orders or gauges. Use one structure's factories throughout a calculation, or supply manual operators as described below.

All Cartesian vectors use the same fixed laboratory axes, with the quantisation axis along ẑ. B is signed: reversing it does not reverse the axes, relabel $m$ or reinterpret a Jones vector's handedness. At B = 0 the chosen axis remains part of the representation convention. A perturbing field b is expressed in the same frame; complex phasors go through zeeman_drives, with $\vec B(t) = \mathrm{Re}[\vec b\, e^{-iΩt}]$.

Conventions that fix signs

Three independent layers fix the sign of every hyperfine amplitude: Condon–Shortley Clebsch–Gordan phases; the electron-first coupling order (nuclear-first sources differ by $(-1)^{I + J - F}$ per state; see Levels.hyperfine_reduction); and the eigenvector sign. Under a whole-column rephasing $W → W D$, operators transform as $X_e → D^† X_e D$, and state vectors and density matrices transform with them: individual entries and their relative phases change, consistently calculated observables and interference predictions do not. Replacing individual eigenvector components by their absolute values is not a valid phase repair.

Each eigenvector's component on its own zero-field label is made positive; if that component is exactly zero, the largest-magnitude component is made positive. For a canonical complete basis with resolved zero-field levels, this gives $W → I$ as $B → 0$. A sign jump can occur when the chosen component crosses zero, so signed amplitudes in a field sweep need not be continuous. For example, the ⁴³Ca⁺ D$_{5/2}$ branch labelled $(F, m_F) = (4, -1)$ has such a crossing near 1.29 mT.

Rabi-flopping measurements determine magnitudes, not coherent relative phases: relative phases between several hand-entered connections must be supplied in one consistent gauge.

Zero field and degenerate branches

At B = 0 the eigenstates are the coupled $|F, m_F⟩$ states and the offsets their Casimir shifts; for a complete canonical basis $W = I$. Where the zero-field $F$ energies within each $m_F$ block are distinct, this is also the continuous limit of the field solutions:

deviation(b) = maximum(abs, eigenvectors(Eigenstructure(ca43, "S_1/2", "D_5/2"; B=b)) - I)
deviation(1e-3u"mT"), deviation(1e-6u"mT")
(0.003832313410352557, 3.832373009379714e-6)

Degenerate zero-field $F$ levels within a block (a species with vanishing hyperfine constants but a nonzero nuclear moment, say) have no unique adiabatic continuation: the finite-field eigenvectors are then fixed mixtures that do not approach the identity, the labels follow a deterministic policy (ascending $F$ among the degenerate levels, by energy order at the field), and the structure makes no continuity claim for them. Diagonalising each exact-$m_F$ block separately keeps different $m$ sectors from mixing at any degeneracy; it does not remove degeneracy within a block.

Partial and reordered bases

The eigenproblem is always solved on complete manifolds. basis(s) selects which labelled eigenstates matrices address, and in which order: a matrix over a partial basis holds the exact matrix elements between the retained eigenstates, and omitting states truncates the model a consumer builds, never the solution. $W$ then has one orthonormal column per basis state over the complete coupled rows, and to_eigenbasis(s, X) — which requires X over coupled_basis(s); an n × n matrix over a partial basis(s) is refused — is the exact projection of a complete-manifold operator onto the retained eigenstates. Scalar queries (state_offset, state_energy, zeeman_shift, transition_amplitude, rabi_frequency, intensity_for_rabi, transition_frequency, zeeman_sensitivity, polarisability) accept any state of coupled_basis(s), not only basis states; matrix queries are indexed by basis(s) alone. iscomplete(s, level) tells whether a manifold is present in full — DrivenTransition and quasienergy_shift require it, as their within-manifold sums would silently change with a dropped state; lindblad_model treats a dropped state as a modelling choice under its open_channels policy.

Projection does not preserve products: for rectangular $W$, $(W^† X W)(W^† Y W) ≠ W^† X Y W$ in general, so squaring projected angular-momentum operators or forming effective interactions through discarded virtual states changes the physics. Exact projected matrix elements do not imply exact truncated dynamics.

partial = Eigenstructure(ca43, [StateSpec("D_5/2 F=4", 3), StateSpec("S_1/2 F=4", 4)]; B=0.5u"mT")
size(eigenvectors(partial)), iscomplete(partial, "S_1/2"), state_offset(partial, StateSpec("S_1/2 F=3", 0))
((64, 2), false, 11400.336299554541 rad μs^-1)

Checking a representation

The eigenvectors have orthonormal columns, $W^† W = I$, and diagonalise the centroid-relative Hamiltonian:

\[W^† H_c W = \operatorname{diag}(\texttt{offsets(s)}).\]

For a whole fine-structure pair, a structure-form coupling equals the transformed species-form coupling. The same identity holds for relative_couplings, each dipole_components matrix, and zeeman_hamiltonian. Coupling scales use the pair's centroid frequency and Einstein A coefficient in both forms.

H = coupled_hamiltonian(s)
residual = maximum(abs, ustrip.(u"rad/µs", to_eigenbasis(s, H) - Diagonal(offsets(s))))
n, ε = beam_vectors(π / 2, π / 4)
C_e = coupling_matrix(s, "S_1/2" => "D_5/2", 1e4u"W/m^2", ε, n)
C_c = coupling_matrix(ca43, coupled_basis(s), "S_1/2" => "D_5/2", 1e4u"W/m^2", ε, n)
rotation = maximum(abs, ustrip.(u"rad/µs", C_e - to_eigenbasis(s, C_c)))
(; residual, rotation)
(residual = 5.4569682106375694e-12, rotation = 1.3877787807814457e-17)

Selecting $F$ labels: rotate, then select

An $F$ level named in a structure-form coupling factory selects the eigenstates carrying that label after the whole fine-structure pair has been rotated: $P^e_{up} (W^† C W) P^e_{lo}$. Rotating a zero-field $F$-restricted operator, $W^† (P_{up} C P_{lo}) W$, is a different operation — the eigenstates have components on other $F$ levels — and the two differ at the tens-of-percent level for the ⁴³Ca⁺ $F = 4 → F = 4$ E2 block at 0.5 mT:

basis = Levels.basis(s)
r4s, r4d = staterange(basis, "S_1/2 F=4"), staterange(basis, "D_5/2 F=4")
selected = relative_couplings(s, "S_1/2 F=4" => "D_5/2 F=4", ε, n)[r4d, r4s]
whole = relative_couplings(s, "S_1/2" => "D_5/2", ε, n)[r4d, r4s]
rotated_restricted = to_eigenbasis(s, relative_couplings(ca43, coupled_basis(s), "S_1/2 F=4" => "D_5/2 F=4", ε, n))[r4d, r4s]
norm(selected - whole) / norm(whole), norm(rotated_restricted - whole) / norm(whole)
(0.0, 0.5735614054730503)

A RelativeFrequency pair is independently a frequency marker, not a restriction on which components a beam drives.

Manual operators

An operator written from zero-field quantum numbers uses coupled_basis(s); to_eigenbasis rotates and, for a partial basis, projects it:

cb = coupled_basis(s)
X = zeros(ComplexF64, length(cb), length(cb)) .* u"rad/µs"
X[stateindex(cb, "D_5/2 F=4", 1), stateindex(cb, "S_1/2 F=4", 0)] = angular(100u"kHz")
C = to_eigenbasis(s, X)
abs(C[stateindex(basis, "D_5/2 F=4", 1), stateindex(basis, "S_1/2 F=4", 0)])
0.3584489251868395 rad μs^-1

A measured coupling between labelled field eigenstates is entered directly over Levels.basis(s). To retain the relative phases set by a beam geometry, scale its generated matrix instead: rabi_normalised(relative_couplings(s, pair, ε, n), Levels.basis(s), probe, Ω0).

For a product-basis operator in one manifold, first form U' * X_p * U with U = Levels.coupling_transform(species(s), level). Its index space is StateBasis(species(s), level); embed that block into coupled_basis(s), then call to_eigenbasis.

A manual HarmonicDrive(X, phase) takes a Hermitian angular-frequency matrix over the structure's eigenbasis and represents X*cos(ω*t + phase). zeeman_drives(s, b) builds these matrices from a laboratory field phasor. quasienergy_shift(s, state_or_pair, ω, drives) requires these drives to be block-diagonal in the fine-structure manifolds, and each probed manifold to be complete and contiguous in the basis. Couplings between manifolds raise an error. ac_zeeman_shift(s, state_or_pair, ω, b) constructs magnetic drives from b. The keyword constructor of DrivenTransition, BandLiouvillian(matrix, …) and quasienergy_shift(H0, H1, …) also support plain-matrix models; the caller supplies a common basis and ordering for their inputs.

Inspecting eigenstates and sweeping fields

eigenvectors(s)[:, stateindex(Levels.basis(s), state)] gives an eigenstate's coupled-basis composition. Summing squared components within an $F$ level gives its population in that level:

v = eigenvectors(s)[:, stateindex(basis, "D_5/2 F=4", 3)]
[(level, sum(abs2, v[staterange(cb, level)])) for level in cb.levels if sum(abs2, v[staterange(cb, level)]) > 1e-6]
4-element Vector{Tuple{HyperfineNumberSpec, Float64}}:
 (D_5/2 F=3, 0.2367258759867565)
 (D_5/2 F=4, 0.6094018208051393)
 (D_5/2 F=5, 0.1502901839174961)
 (D_5/2 F=6, 0.0035821192906082384)

A RelativeFrequency(s, probe, δ) stores the laser frequency determined at construction. Reuse it across structures for a fixed laser; rebuild it at each field for a fixed detuning from the moving resonance.

Physical dipole components

dipole_components returns the three E1 operator components in coulomb metres. Contracting them with the beam geometry recovers rabi_frequency; its docstring defines the reduced-element and interaction sign conventions. Rate data fixes the magnitude of each reduced element, but supplies no relative sign between different fine-structure pairs.

sp = Eigenstructure(ca43, "S_1/2", "P_1/2"; B=0.5u"mT")
D = dipole_components(sp, "S_1/2" => "P_1/2")
lo, up = StateSpec("S_1/2 F=4", 4), StateSpec("P_1/2 F=4", 3)
intensity = 1.0u"W/m^2"
E0 = sqrt(2 * intensity / (u"ε0" * u"c"))
εσ = [1.0, -im, 0.0] / sqrt(2)
g = dipole_geometry(εσ)
Ω_contracted = uconvert(u"rad/µs", E0 / u"ħ" * sum(g[q+2] * D[q+2][stateindex(Levels.basis(sp), up), stateindex(Levels.basis(sp), lo)] for q in -1:1))
Ω_contracted ≈ rabi_frequency(sp, lo => up, intensity, εσ, [0, 0, 1.0])
true

The components are the extension interface for custom second-order sums. The S$_{1/2}$ polarisability at 674 nm from the explicit S → P channels plus the lumped static remainder of the data, with the structure's energies in the denominators:

s3 = Eigenstructure(sr88, "S_1/2", "P_1/2", "P_3/2"; B=0.5u"mT")
b3 = Levels.basis(s3)
state = StateSpec("S_1/2", 1//2)
λ = 674.025u"nm"
ħω = photon_energy(λ)
_, ε_lin = beam_vectors(0.4, 0.6)
w = Levels.polarisation_weights(ε_lin)
function custom_sum()
    α = level_polarisability(sr88, "S_1/2").static_scalar
    for upper in ("P_1/2", "P_3/2"), (k, t) in enumerate(b3)
        t.level == NoHyperfineNumberSpec(upper) || continue
        Dq = dipole_components(s3, "S_1/2" => upper)
        Δ = state_energy(s3, t) - state_energy(s3, state)
        for q in -1:1
            d = Dq[q+2][k, stateindex(b3, state)]
            α += uconvert(u"C*m^2/V", abs2(d) * (w[q+2] / (Δ - ħω) + w[2-q] / (Δ + ħω)))
        end
    end
    α
end
custom_sum() / polarisability(s3, state, λ, ε_lin)
0.9999999999999996
