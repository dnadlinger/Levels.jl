Conventions and examples

Frequencies, rates and energies

QuantityConvention
Optical and trap frequencies, Rabi frequencies and detuningsAngular frequency, returned in rad µs⁻¹ (u"rad/µs")
Einstein A, population decay, cooling and heating ratesEvents or quanta per time; no factor of 2π
Laser linewidthLorentzian FWHM in angular-frequency units
Hyperfine constants a, bEnergies: multiply a quoted cyclic frequency by h
Hamiltonian matricesH/ħ, in angular-frequency units
Jump operatorsSquare root of a rate
Electric-dipole componentsCoulomb metres (dipole_components)

Angular frequencies carry Unitful's rad as a marker. The marker is dimensionless and therefore soft: Unitful identifies Hz, s⁻¹ and rad s⁻¹ with each other and cannot detect a missing factor of 2π, and a sum of a rad µs⁻¹ and a plain µs⁻¹ quantity silently drops the marker. Use angular(50u"MHz") for a stated cyclic frequency, and cyclic(ω) to display an angular result in Hz. Do not apply either conversion to an Einstein A or a heating rate, which stay plain inverse times. These conversions change the physical convention; uconvert only changes the units.

level_energy(species, level) and state_energy(s, state) return energies in joules relative to the species' energy reference. state_offset(s, state) and offsets(s) return angular-frequency offsets from each fine-structure centroid. Use transition_frequency(s, lower => upper) for an interval: it keeps the centroid and offset terms separate, preserving small splittings within a manifold.

saturation_intensity(species, lower => upper) returns I_0 = ħω³/(6πc²τ), with τ the total lifetime of the upper level. For a fine-structure E1 pair, a component driven at unit geometric amplitude then has |Ω|² = C² A/τ, with C its Clebsch–Gordan coefficient and A the pair's Einstein coefficient. On a closed stretched transition, |Ω| = Γ = 1/τ. This is twice the two-level I_sat of the s = I/I_sat = 2|Ω|²/Γ² saturation-parameter convention. The saturation parameter of an individual component also depends on its angular and geometric strength.

States, fields and the structure

Levels accept strings such as "D_5/2"; hyperfine levels include an F suffix, for example "D_5/2 F=4". StateSpec(level, m) uses mJ for a fine-structure level and mF for a hyperfine level. Invalid magnetic projections are rejected. StateBasis(species, levels...) expands each level into increasing-m substates; for a hyperfine species a fine-structure shorthand expands all its F levels. Explicit state lists fix any desired ordering.

Transitions use lower => upper pairs. The static magnetic field is specified in exactly one place: the constructor of an Eigenstructure, Eigenstructure(species, basis; B), with B a signed scalar flux density along the quantisation axis ẑ (zero allowed, no default). The structure solves every fine-structure manifold the basis touches and is the atom all further calculations take:

using Levels, Unitful
B = 0.5u"mT"
s = Eigenstructure(ca43, "S_1/2", "D_5/2"; B)
Eigenstructure(S_1/2, D_5/2; 64 states; B = 0.5 mT)

Species forms use zero-field states, for example transition_amplitude(ca43, pair) or polarisability(ca43, state, ω). Structure forms evaluate the labelled eigenstates at field(s), for example transition_amplitude(s, pair) or polarisability(s, state, ω). Model constructors take a structure: LightShiftCoefficients(s, laser), DrivenTransition(s, probe; …) and lindblad_model(s, scheme).

Operators from a species form use the supplied coupled basis; operators from a structure form use its field eigenbasis, indexed by Levels.basis(s). Bases and representations explains the eigenstate labels, partial bases, phase conventions and construction of manual operators.

Beam geometry and signs

beam_vectors returns (direction, ε): a propagation direction and complex polarisation. The field convention is E(t) = Re[ε E₀ exp(-im*ω*t)]; a right-circular beam along ẑ drives Δm = +1. The complex phase of the Rabi frequency is retained. The coupling Hamiltonian is (C + C')/2, with C containing angular Rabi frequencies in the upper-row, lower-column block. The physical dipole operator behind the Rabi frequency, and the sign convention of −d·E, are documented at dipole_components.

Detuning is laser minus atomic frequency. RelativeFrequency(level_pair, offset) uses the zero-field level interval as its marker. The component form RelativeFrequency(s, state_pair, detuning) folds the exact component shift at the structure's field into that marker; it stores the frequency determined then and does not follow the component into a structure at another field. A light-shift sweep's δ overrides the marker offset; it is not necessarily detuning from a particular Zeeman component.

Spherical channels use ordinary Julia indexing: index q + rank + 1, hence q + 2 for E1 and q + 3 for E2. Fock occupation n starts at zero; num_fock counts retained states, so the maximum occupation is num_fock - 1.

Periodically driven transitions

The structure supplies the static energies; beam and field geometry supply the laser coupling and ac drive matrices:

using Levels.PeriodicDynamics
probe = StateSpec("S_1/2 F=4", 0) => StateSpec("D_5/2 F=4", 1)
direction, ε = beam_vectors(π/2, π/4)

# Structure-form matrices are in the field eigenbasis of s already.
C = coupling_matrix(s, "S_1/2" => "D_5/2", 1e6u"W/m^2", ε, direction)
drives = zeeman_drives(s, [10.0, 0.0, 0.0]u"µT")
dt = DrivenTransition(s, probe; drive_frequency=angular(50u"MHz"), coupling=C, drives)

For a measured carrier Rabi frequency, use rabi_normalised to scale the structure's relative coupling matrix. For an operator written in the coupled basis, use to_eigenbasis before passing it to a model; see Manual operators.

Treat species, bases, structures, schemes and models as read-only after construction, including their arrays: accessors return the stored data directly.

Numerical methods and physical results

perturbative = sideband_rabi(dt, DressedStates(
    harmonic_cutoff=8, num_time_samples=256); sideband=1)
(Ω = 0.0004156429180301143 rad μs^-1, δ_res = 314.15901940523185 rad μs^-1, amp = (-1.627929561986985e-15 - 0.00020782145901505715im) rad μs^-1, carrier = 0.15926799418967494 rad μs^-1)
reference = sideband_rabi(dt, Monodromy(reltol=1e-10); sideband=1)
(Ω = 0.0004156302543580532 rad μs^-1, δ_res = 314.15839011060353 rad μs^-1)

Increase the harmonic cutoff and time sampling to check convergence. A cutoff K retains -K:K, or 2K+1 harmonic blocks. Monodromy includes the laser nonperturbatively within the model; its numerical accuracy is controlled by the ODE and avoided-crossing search. A complex perturbative coupling amplitude is available only from the dressed-state calculation.

Quasienergy shifts under harmonic driving use the same explicit method choice, on the same structure. Ac Zeeman shifts are one application:

drives = zeeman_drives(s, [10.0, 0.0, 0.0]u"µT")
shift = quasienergy_shift(s, probe, angular(50u"MHz"),
                         drives, PerturbationTheory())
(shift = -0.00024595525405480216 rad μs^-1, lower = -7.127198628080402e-6 rad μs^-1, upper = -0.00025308245268288257 rad μs^-1)
check = quasienergy_shift(s, probe, angular(50u"MHz"), drives, Floquet())
(shift = -0.0002459537462655703 rad μs^-1, lower = -7.127200660761446e-6 rad μs^-1, upper = -0.00025308094692633176 rad μs^-1)

The pair form returns (; shift, lower, upper); the state form returns a scalar shift. quasienergy_shift_contributions breaks down the perturbative sum by intermediate state.

Custom HarmonicDrives can represent other interactions. These structure forms require drives at one frequency, block-diagonal in the fine-structure manifolds, with each probed manifold complete and contiguous in the basis. Couplings between manifolds raise an error; a general model may instead use the quasienergy_shift(H0, H1, ω, i) matrix form with full static energies.

For magnetic fields, ac_zeeman_shift accepts the field phasor directly and constructs the drives:

ac_zeeman_shift(s, probe, angular(50u"MHz"), [10.0, 0.0, 0.0]u"µT") == shift
true

For sideband +1, the dressed-state resonance minus the drive frequency is the difference of the two dressed levels' ac shifts. Floquet() evaluates those same shifts; PerturbationTheory() approximates them to second order. The Monodromy resonance also includes probe-induced light shifts:

perturbative.δ_res - dt.drive_frequency
-0.0002459537474805984 rad μs^-1

Cooling calculations

cooling_metrics offers several relaxation observables. Adiabatic elimination gives a rate-equation exponential time constant; SaturatedRates and IntegratedTransient give an integral relaxation time for a specified initial state, from a rate equation and a joint model respectively; LiouvillianSpectrum gives an asymptotic relaxation time; SemiclassicalRates gives the local rate of classical motion at a given energy. They coincide for a single exponential and can differ for a hot ion or a transient with several timescales. The Optical Bloch reference describes their assumptions and numerical controls.

Solver wrappers return unitful rates and times. Plain-matrix BandLiouvillian constructors use a declared time_unit (µs by default); model-based constructors inherit the model's scale. Numerical engines use Float64/ComplexF64.
